# Offer Stage Protocol

> Use when the artifact is an offer architecture, value ladder, pricing grid, or guarantee structure. The 4-lens roster covers structural offer engineering (Hormozi), sophistication fit (Schwartz), trust mechanics (Cialdini), and strategic-fit (Christensen).

## Round 1 — Independent diagnosis questions

1. **Value Equation calibration**: score the offer on DO × PL / TD × ES (1-10 each). Compute composite V. Is V > 1.5 (defensible premium) or below (offer is structurally weak)?
2. **Grand Slam construction**: are obstacles enumerated FIRST, then mapped to bundle components? Or are components listed without obstacle-mapping (= padding)?
3. **Guarantee architecture**: is the guarantee unconditional, conditional, performance-based, or implied? Is it better-than-money-back? Is the activation point real or theatrical?
4. **Risk reversal stack**: how many risk reversals are layered? Are they composing or contradicting?
5. **Sophistication fit**: is the offer leading with mechanism (Stage 4-5 fit) or escalation (Stage 1-3 fit)? Does the audience match?
6. **Trust signals**: which Cialdini principles are activated (reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity)? Which are honest vs manipulative?
7. **JTBD completion**: does the offer complete the buyer's full job, or stop at the Big Hire and externalize the Little Hire?
8. **First-win design**: is there a tangible artifact in the first 10-25% of delivery time? Or does the buyer pay upfront and wait?
9. **Pricing anchor**: what does the price anchor against? Is the anchor real (competitor / cost-of-doing-nothing / ROI math) or rhetorical?

## Round 2 — Debate prompts

### Hormozi ↔ Christensen: "Stack bonuses vs purpose-brand discipline"
- Hormozi default: stack bonuses to push perceived value past price.
- Christensen default: a purpose-brand defines what it REFUSES to do. Stacking dilutes refusal; refusal IS the moat.
- Surface: where is the offer accumulating bonuses that violate the purpose-brand?

### Hormozi ↔ Schwartz: "Premium price defended by anchor vs by mechanism"
- Hormozi default: anchor against expensive competitor; price-by-comparison.
- Schwartz default: at Stage-4 sophistication, anchor-by-comparison gets discounted by buyers. Defend price by mechanism specificity (production cost transparency, named components).
- Surface: is the offer price defensible *if the buyer ignores the comparison*? If not, the price is rhetorical.

### Cialdini ↔ Hormozi: "Scarcity authenticity vs scarcity as conversion lever"
- Cialdini default: scarcity must be operationally true; manufactured scarcity destroys trust on second purchase.
- Hormozi default: scarcity is a conversion lever; deploy it.
- Surface: is the scarcity (cupos, deadline, capacity) defensible under buyer scrutiny? Or is it a Hormozi instinct dressed up as math?

### Polish candidates (REQUIRED for offer stage)

Produce **3 offer-architecture variants**:
- **A) Conservative** — minimal changes to current offer, fixing only convergent decision-rule violations
- **B) Aggressive** — premium pricing pushed + bonus stack + outsized guarantee (Hormozi-led)
- **C) Disruptor** — opt-in revenue-tied / performance-based / lead-magnet-heavy (Christensen + Schwartz led)

### Panel verdict

Recommend ONE variant + the conditions under which to test the others.

# Learner Profiling: What to Assess and How to Adapt

## Overview
The variables that actually change optimal learning strategy, how to assess them in a brief interview, and how each answer deterministically changes the roadmap. Replaces debunked learning-styles matching with evidence-backed individual difference variables.

## Variable 1: Prior knowledge and current competence level
Prior knowledge is the single strongest predictor of learning outcomes and meaningfully changes which instructional supports work best. Guidance that helps novices (worked examples, explicit steps) becomes redundant or harmful for experienced learners (expertise reversal effect).

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "Pick one. (A) New: I can't reliably do basic tasks yet. (B) Basic: I can follow tutorials but struggle solo. (C) Intermediate: I can complete routine tasks solo. (D) Advanced: I can solve unfamiliar problems and explain my choices."
- "Name one concrete thing you can do now without looking anything up, and one thing you cannot do yet."

ADAPTATION RULES:
- A (New): Tight sequencing, direct instruction, worked examples, immediate practice. Gate progress with mastery checks before adding complexity.
- B/C (Basic/Intermediate): Example-problem pairs, then fade steps. Add spaced retrieval and interleaving once basics stabilize.
- D (Advanced): Compress explanations, prioritize challenging problems, varied contexts, higher-level feedback on strategy. Reduce scaffolds.

## Variable 2: Target outcome and transfer distance
"Learning" is not one target. Retention and transfer vary by context similarity. Roadmaps for far transfer need varied practice and preparation for future learning, not just repetition.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "What is the main win condition? (A) Pass an assessment. (B) Do a real task at work. (C) Build a portfolio project. (D) Explain and reason about the topic at depth. (E) General curiosity and breadth."
- "Where will you use this, and under what constraints (time pressure, tools, stakes)?"

ADAPTATION RULES:
- A (Assessment): Emphasize retrieval practice with timed sets, error logs, spaced review.
- B/C (Work task/Portfolio): Center on authentic tasks and projects, keep concept drills as maintenance layer.
- D/E (Understanding/Curiosity): Build concept backbone first, add contrast cases and explanation prompts.

## Variable 3: Time budget, energy, and schedule reliability
Distributed practice reliably improves long-term retention. Mastery learning explicitly treats time allowed and perseverance as key variables.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "Realistically, how many minutes per day and days per week can you dedicate? What is your longest uninterrupted block?"
- "Is your schedule (A) stable, (B) unpredictable week to week?"

ADAPTATION RULES:
- Low time or low energy: Micro-sessions (10-20 min) built around one retrieval prompt plus one application. Automatic spaced reviews.
- High time and stable: Longer synthesis sessions (projects, mixed problem sets, reflection) and deliberate interleaving across subskills.
- Unpredictable: Define "minimum viable day" that preserves spaced retrieval so progress does not reset.

## Variable 4: Self-regulation and metacognitive habits
Self-regulated learning is a reliable contributor to achievement. Adaptive scaffolding plus formative assessment improves regulation and outcomes.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "When you study now, what do you actually do most? Pick up to two. (A) Reread/rewatch. (B) Take notes. (C) Do practice problems/projects. (D) Self-test without notes. (E) Review errors and retry later."
- "Do you keep any system for tracking mistakes and revisiting them?"

ADAPTATION RULES:
- Low SRL signals (mostly A/B, no tracking): Enforced structure with short cycles, frequent low-stakes quizzes, explicit reflection prompts, error log that drives next tasks.
- High SRL signals (C/D/E, has tracking): More learner control and fewer forced checkpoints, but periodic mastery checks to avoid illusions of competence.

## Variable 5: Motivation source and persistence risk
Autonomous motivation predicts better engagement and persistence. Expectancy-value models link effort to perceived value and expectancy for success.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "What is driving this right now? (A) Curiosity/enjoyment. (B) Career/credential. (C) Deadline or external pressure. (D) Fixing a specific pain point."
- "Confidence to succeed in this topic, 0-10. Biggest dropout risk, one phrase."

ADAPTATION RULES:
- Mostly C or low confidence: Fast visible wins, tight milestones, accountability hooks, reduce early ambiguity.
- Mostly A/D: Exploration lanes, optional extensions, learner choice of projects, anchored to retrieval and feedback loops.

## Variable 6: Need for structure versus autonomy
Minimally guided approaches are generally less effective for novices. Learner control helps when learners have sufficient prior knowledge and self-regulation.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "Which plan do you follow better? (A) Step-by-step path with required checkpoints. (B) A menu of options where I choose each week. (C) Hybrid: guided core plus optional branches."
- "When stuck: (A) I want a hint fast. (B) I prefer to struggle awhile first."

ADAPTATION RULES:
- A (Step-by-step): Enforce sequencing, mastery thresholds, guided practice, then fade as performance rises.
- B (Menu): Offer branching choices, but restrict choices early if prior knowledge is low.
- Hint-fast: Add worked examples, checklists, immediate feedback.
- Struggle-first: Use "productive attempt then feedback" while keeping tasks inside solvable-with-support band.

## Variable 7: Preferred engagement mode (as adherence lever, NOT learning style)
No solid basis for matching instruction to declared style, but giving learners some choice improves motivation and performance.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "What will you actually do consistently? Rank your top two: reading, watching, building/making, drilling problems, discussion/teaching."
- "What format have you stuck with for at least a month in the past?"

ADAPTATION RULES:
- Pick preferred mode as default on-ramp, but force the same learning loop every time: brief input, immediate practice, retrieval check, feedback, spaced revisit.
- If prefers watching: design pause-and-predict checkpoints and post-video problem sets.
- If prefers building: drive concepts from project constraints plus short retrieval quizzes.

## Variable 8: Feedback access and accountability environment
Large tutoring gains are closely tied to rapid individualized feedback and corrective practice.

ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS:
- "Do you have a human reviewer (mentor, peer, manager)? (A) Yes, weekly or faster. (B) Yes, but slower. (C) No."
- "What can give you objective feedback: tests, unit tests, rubrics, automated graders, real users?"

ADAPTATION RULES:
- A/B (Has reviewer): Short submit-review-revise cycles, increase task authenticity earlier.
- C (No reviewer): Self-grading infrastructure: answer keys, checklists, automated tests, frequent retrieval quizzes to simulate tutor checkpoints. Use AI as rubric-based second rater with triangulation.

## Complete 8-question interview script
1. Current level (A/B/C/D) + one can-do, one cannot-do example
2. Primary goal (A-E) + where/how they will use it
3. Time: minutes/day, days/week, longest block, schedule stability
4. Current study behavior: pick up to two (A-E) + error tracking yes/no
5. Motivation (A-D) + confidence 0-10 + biggest dropout risk
6. Structure preference (A-C) + stuck behavior (hint-fast vs struggle-first)
7. Engagement mode: rank top two + longest-sustained format
8. Feedback access (A-C) + available objective feedback tools

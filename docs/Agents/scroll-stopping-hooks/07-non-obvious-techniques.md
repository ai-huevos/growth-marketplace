# Non-Obvious Techniques: Cross-Disciplinary Hook and Angle Methods

## Overview
Seven techniques imported from film editing, screenwriting, improv comedy, inventive problem-solving, constrained writing, and qualitative research. Each produces hooks and angles that standard copywriting frameworks miss.

## Kuleshov Pair Hook (Inference Cut)
**Origin:** Film editing / montage theory (Lev Kuleshov).
**How it works:** Write the opener as two adjacent "shots." Beat A is under-specified (neutral claim); Beat B is a context cue that forces reinterpretation of A. The audience generates the meaning in the gap between the two beats.
**Example:** "I deleted 90% of my hooks. My retention went up — because the second sentence does the persuading."
**Risk:** If A is too empty or B too unrelated, it feels random. The linkage must feel intentional.
**When to use:** When you want the audience to feel they "discovered" the insight rather than being told it.

## Bomb-Under-the-Table Opener (Suspense via Early Disclosure)
**Origin:** Screenwriting / suspense craft (Hitchcock's suspense principle, documented by David Bordwell).
**How it works:** Instead of hiding the key info, reveal the stake in line one. Show an ordinary action continuing anyway, so the audience watches the mundane under threat. Add a clock to convert information into suspense.
**Example:** "This prompting habit makes models sound smarter while increasing errors. I used it in client decks for six months."
**Risk:** Weak or exaggerated bombs train distrust. The revealed stake must be real and verifiable.
**When to use:** When you have a genuinely surprising finding or mistake to share. Works especially well for educational/cautionary content.

## Status-Transaction Tilt (Improv Comedy)
**Origin:** Keith Johnstone's improv/comedy status work.
**How it works:** Open with a violated status expectation — novice corrects expert, tool outranks user, audience refuses guru. Name the new relationship in one clarifying line. Heighten once, then cash out the lesson.
**Example:** "I paid $10,000 for advice. The consultant told me to stop taking consultant advice — and then proved it with my analytics."
**Risk:** Without specificity, it reads as performative swagger. Without humility, it triggers backlash.
**When to use:** When you have a story where the expected power dynamic was reversed.

## Found-Artifact Cold Open (Primary Source First)
**Origin:** Narrative audio/investigative storytelling (This American Life) + evidence-first OSINT posture.
**How it works:** Lead with a legible artifact — a metric, receipt, email subject, log line, screenshot — that implies a story. Add one orientation sentence without fully interpreting. Return to the artifact later as evidence.
**Example:** "Retention drop: −38% by second 12. That single line changed how I write intros."
**Risk:** Missing context confuses. Fake "receipts" destroy trust. Artifacts can create privacy issues.
**When to use:** When you have real data, a real screenshot, or a real moment that tells the story better than summary.

## TRIZ Contradiction Matrix (Tradeoff Dissolving)
**Origin:** Inventive problem-solving, TRIZ (Genrich Altshuller).
**How it works:** Write a contradiction: "We want more X, but that worsens Y." Apply inventive principles as lenses (segmentation, inversion, intermediary, doing it in advance) to generate one angle per principle. Turn the contradiction into a hook by naming the hidden cost.
**Example:** "Hooks faster" contradiction: speed ↑ originality ↓ → angles: modular hook parts (segmentation), write sentence two first (inversion), borrow audience language as a proxy (intermediary).
**Risk:** Outputs can feel mechanical if not translated into concrete, lived scenarios.
**When to use:** For angle generation when standard frameworks produce similar outputs. Forces structurally novel angles by attacking the hidden tradeoff.

## OuLiPo Lens Stack (Constraint Collision)
**Origin:** Constrained writing / OuLiPo literary group (Queneau, Perec, Le Lionnais, 1960).
**How it works:** Pick one conceptual constraint (lab protocol, legal cross-exam, postmortem, therapist intake) and one formal constraint (ban a keyword, limit sentences, forbid adjectives). Generate variants under these constraints. Keep the best structure, rewrite for clarity.
**Example:** Postmortem lens + "no 'you' in first 2 lines": "Incident: the hooks that felt 'natural' underperformed. Root cause: they optimized politeness, not attention."
**Risk:** Over-constraint yields novel-but-unusable output. Under-constraint yields generic output. Sweet spot is 2 simultaneous constraints.
**When to use:** When you're stuck in a pattern and need to force yourself out of familiar structures.

## Adjacent-Possible Objection Mining (KJ Clustering)
**Origin:** Qualitative synthesis (KJ/affinity diagramming, Jiro Kawakita) + "adjacent possible" concept (Stuart Kauffman / Steven Johnson).
**How it works:** Turn audience responses (comments, DMs, objections) into atomic cards. Cluster with KJ method into 6-12 theme piles labeled in audience language. For each pile, generate adjacent angles by mutating exactly one dimension (time, identity, stakes, mechanism, evidence type). Write hooks that mirror original phrasing before reframing.
**Example:** Theme "Hooks feel manipulative" → adjacent angles: consent hooks (state why early), editing hooks (cut setup, don't add hype), evidence hooks (artifact-first), stakes hooks (disclose real downside).
**Risk:** Bad clustering yields noise. DM mining requires anonymization and aggregation.
**When to use:** When you have audience feedback (comments, objections, questions) and want to mine it systematically for non-obvious content angles.

## Key Takeaways
- These techniques work because they change the attention structure, not just the words
- Each can be combined with standard frameworks for amplified distinctiveness
- The best use case: run standard angle frameworks first, then apply 1-2 non-obvious techniques to the most promising angles to push them further
- Every technique requires real substance — they amplify authentic expertise and backfire on empty claims

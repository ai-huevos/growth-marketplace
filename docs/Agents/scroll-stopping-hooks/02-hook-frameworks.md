# Proven Hook Frameworks From Practitioners

## Overview
Battle-tested hook structures from creators with documented track records (50K+ followers, verified engagement data, 2022-2025). Each framework maps to specific psychological mechanisms and platforms.

## Deliverable + Objection Smasher
**Structure:** "How to [desired outcome]: (without [common barrier/objection])"
**Real example:** "How to build your first 2 income streams: (without knowing how to code)"
**Why it works:** Pairs curiosity gap with self-relevance. The objection clause pre-answers "not for me" in the first screenful.
**Best platform:** X threads, LinkedIn — because the first screen must promise value while removing the reader's primary excuse.
**Weakness:** If the objection removal is too marketing-y ("without lifting a finger"), it reads as clickbait.
**Source:** Justin Welsh, Saturday Solopreneur, 2022.

## Eye-Catching Numbers Opener
**Structure:** [Specific metric] + [constraints] → Here are the [N] steps/lessons
**Real example:** "Last week my little one-person business crossed $1.3M in revenue. It took 810 days, I ran zero paid ads… Here are the 14 steps…"
**Why it works:** Numbers compress credibility instantly. Specific metrics create curiosity gap + specificity bias simultaneously.
**Best platform:** X and LinkedIn — numbers work as fast credibility in text-first feeds.
**Weakness:** Numbers trigger skepticism unless the next line rapidly turns focus to reader benefit or replicable process.
**Source:** Justin Welsh, Saturday Solopreneur, 2022.

## Juxtaposition (Before/After Gap)
**Structure:** "I was [baseline]. Now [result]. What changed? [key behavior]. Here's [process]."
**Real example:** "I made $0 online in 2019. 3 years later: … What changed? I started writing online every day."
**Why it works:** Contrast is instantly legible and naturally creates a curiosity gap ("how did that happen?").
**Best platform:** X threads, Instagram/TikTok educational content.
**Weakness:** If the delta feels unattainable or the causal claim oversimplified, the audience discounts ("survivorship bias").
**Source:** Justin Welsh, Saturday Solopreneur, 2022.

## Relatable Enemy → Hero → Gasoline (LinkedIn Above-the-Fold)
**Structure:** Line 1: The {enemy} is {negativity}. Line 2: The {hero} is {strong positive}. Line 3: And I {gasoline}. {teaser question}?
**Real example:** "The 9 to 5 is getting pummeled." / "The great resignation is growing faster than ever." / "And I love it. Why?"
**Why it works:** Creates identity signaling + cognitive dissonance in 3 lines that fit above LinkedIn's "see more" fold (~210 chars).
**Best platform:** LinkedIn specifically — the 3-line structure maps directly to the "see more" choke point.
**Weakness:** If the "enemy" isn't truly shared by the audience, it polarizes without building trust.
**Source:** Justin Welsh, Anatomy of a Viral LinkedIn Post, 2022.

## Trailer Stack (LinkedIn See-More Optimization)
**Structure:** Line 1: pattern-break. Line 2: context/credibility cue. Line 3: explicit promise before "see more."
**Real example:** Hook visible before "…see more" button: "Here are 2 big learnings and some advice:"
**Why it works:** Treats the "see more" fold as a hard attention choke point and optimizes all 3 visible lines.
**Best platform:** LinkedIn — reported 232,471 views, 2,161 interactions, 461 comments on the example post.
**Weakness:** If Line 1 is too polarizing without trustworthy context in Line 2, it attracts hate-reads.
**Source:** Justin Welsh, "My Writing Process for 162.597M+ Impressions On LinkedIn," 2022.

## StoryTweet 3-Act Micro-Story
**Structure:** Introduction (hook) → Explanation (meat) → Action (climax/takeaway).
**Real example:** "I used to be terrified of telling stories…" (with visible engagement counts).
**Why it works:** Narrative momentum creates an open loop that holds attention until payoff.
**Best platform:** X and TikTok/IG voiceover education.
**Weakness:** Fails when the story has no meaningful "turn" — no surprise, no lesson, no tension.
**Source:** Kieran Drew, Kit post "How to tell stories on Twitter," ~2023.

## Negative Checklist Hook ("Red Flags")
**Structure:** "How to [goal]. Avoid these [N] red flags: ↓"
**Real example:** "How to hire A-players every time. Avoid these 15 red flags: ↓ ↓ ↓" (239 reactions, 83 comments, 548K followers).
**Why it works:** List scanning + loss aversion ("don't mess this up") fits professional feeds. "N red flags" promises completeness.
**Best platform:** LinkedIn and X.
**Weakness:** Overuse turns a brand cynical. Invites argument over edge cases unless scoped.
**Source:** Codie A. Sanchez, LinkedIn, ~2023.

## Curation Thread ("I did the work so you don't have to")
**Structure:** "I did {time/effort-heavy work} so you don't have to. Here are {N} {curated items}."
**Real example:** "Over the past 10 years, I have read over 500 business books… Save yourself the time and just read these 10."
**Why it works:** Trades on time savings and perceived effort. Curation implies filtering, which signals expertise.
**Best platform:** X and LinkedIn educational content.
**Weakness:** Fails when too broad or un-opinionated — without sharp filtering criteria, it reads as a generic roundup.
**Source:** Nicolas Cole, LinkedIn, ~2022-2023.

## Six Single-Sentence Opener Types
**Structure:** Choose one type, keep it one sentence, optimize solely to earn sentence two:
1. Moment-in-time ("Last Tuesday at 2pm, I watched…")
2. Controversial opinion ("Morning routines are productivity cosplay")
3. Vulnerable statement ("I almost quit after day 6")
4. Weird/unique insight ("The best hooks don't hook — they mirror")
5. Strong declarative ("The single most important part of everything you write: your first sentence")
6. Thought-provoking question ("What if your content strategy is the reason you're stuck?")
**Best platform:** Cross-platform — format-agnostic decision tree.
**Weakness:** Category mismatch creates bait (vulnerable opener without a lesson; controversial without evidence).
**Source:** Dickie Bush, X thread, April 2022.

## Key Takeaways
- Most proven frameworks combine 2-3 psychological mechanisms
- Platform-specific frameworks (LinkedIn trailer stack) outperform generic cross-platform hooks
- Every framework has a documented weakness — the AI must avoid the failure mode, not just copy the structure
- Specificity and real numbers appear in nearly every high-performing framework
- The best hooks earn "line 2" — that's the only job

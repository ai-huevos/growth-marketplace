# Anti-Patterns: Hooks That Fail, AI Tells, and Outdated Platform Advice

## Overview
Three failure categories: hooks audiences are fatigued by, patterns that signal AI-generated content, and platform advice that's now counterproductive.

## Hook Fatigue Patterns

### "Stop scrolling" / "Watch till the end"
Retention-begging openers that instruct attention rather than earn it. Increasingly policed — platforms reduce reach for accounts that game engagement signals.
**Replacement:** Start with proof, not a plea. Payoff glimpse → constraint → first step.

### "If you know me, you know…"
Assumes a warm, loyal audience. Fails because recommendation surfaces deliver most views to non-followers who have no prior context.
**Replacement:** Situation-first: "If you're in [specific role + scenario + constraint], here's the [specific lever] that changes your outcome."

### "Besties, I don't know who needs to hear this…"
Faux-intimacy that's become a recognizable pre-pitch scent. Audiences quickly infer they're being sold to.
**Replacement:** Concrete confession: "I wasted $X / Y hours because I misunderstood Z. Here's the 10-second fix."

### "I quit my $X job to…"
Big-number transformation flex. Creators explicitly skip these posts now. Bandwagon hooks flood feeds with near-identical templates.
**Replacement:** "Small bet with receipts" — constrained experiment + one verifiable metric + non-obvious mechanism.

### "Steal my…" / "10X your…"
Pseudo-generosity and guarantee language. Recognized as guru-template content.
**Replacement:** "Here's what worked for [specific context] — and the 2 situations where it fails." Conditionality signals expertise.

## AI Detection Patterns

### Vague teaser openers with no payload
"This one means a lot…" / "I've kept this quiet…" — high-probability intrigue starters that are "safe" across topics. Creators call these first-line tells.
**Countermeasure:** First line must contain the topic noun + one concrete detail (number, constraint, or named mechanism).

### "Let's dive in" + list-dump structure
"Let's dive in. Here are 7 powerful strategies…" — default "helpful list mode" that creators cite as a common AI smell.
**Countermeasure:** No list until after one concrete example. Ban "let's dive in," "here's the breakdown." Require opening choice from: contrast, scene, observation, or direct question.

### Lexical overrepresentation of "AI words"
"Delve," "intricate," "underscore," "leverage," "unlock," "game-changer," "level up," "dive into," "master." Computational linguistics documents rapid increases in these words linked to LLM usage.
**Countermeasure:** Maintain a blacklist. Prefer concrete verbs (cut, tested, shipped, broke) over abstract (delve, explore, leverage). Rewrite if blacklisted word appears in first line.

### Em-dash addiction and template rhetoric
"Not X — but Y" stacked multiple times. Frequent em dashes and balanced frames.
**Countermeasure:** Max one em dash per hook. Prefer periods. Forbid repeated "not X but Y" frames.

### Low-burstiness neutrality
"In today's fast-paced world, it's important to…" — overly even tone, vague essay framing. AI detectors exploit predictability.
**Countermeasure:** Force stance + specificity. Hook must include a falsifiable claim OR a contrast plus one concrete mechanism.

## Outdated Platform Advice

### TikTok: "Keep everything ultra-short"
**What changed:** 2024 Creator Rewards Program shifted toward longer original content (>1 minute). "Play duration" and "search value" are core metrics.
**Current practice:** Build hooks that sustain >60 seconds. Show payoff early, deliver micro-payoffs every 5-10 seconds.

### Instagram: "Grow by reposting what's already working"
**What changed:** April 2024 recommendations update targets serial reposters/aggregators. Reposts removed from recommendations; replaced with originals.
**Current practice:** If repurposing, materially transform. Hook should signal new value immediately.

### YouTube: "Scale by templating mass-produced videos"
**What changed:** July 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content." Mass-produced or template-like videos with little variation explicitly defined.
**Current practice:** Reuse formats but each video needs substantively different substance. Hook should name the unique angle upfront.

### Threads/LinkedIn: "Farm replies with shallow engagement bait"
**What changed:** Platforms acknowledged engagement-bait problems. LinkedIn enforcement against automated comments. Threads experiments showed rage-bait spreading "almost too successfully."
**Current practice:** Design for meaningful reply, not maximum replies. Hook with a clear claim + bounded question inviting nuance.

## Complete Blacklist for Hook Generation
These phrases/patterns must NEVER appear in generated hooks:
- "In today's…" / "In the world of…"
- "Game-changer" / "unlock" / "level up" / "dive into" / "master" / "delve"
- "Here's the thing:" / "The truth is:" / "What if I told you…"
- "Did you know?" / "Let's dive in" / "Here's the breakdown"
- "Stop scrolling" / "Watch till the end" / "Wait for it"
- "I don't know who needs to hear this but…"
- "It's important to note that…" / "While there are many approaches…"
- "Powerful, effective, and transformative" (or any 3-adjective stack)
- Round numbers used as fake specificity (100, 1000, 10000)
- "The ONE trick that will 10X your…"

## Key Takeaways
- Hook fatigue is real and accelerating — patterns that worked 12-18 months ago now signal "skip"
- AI detection is primarily about predictability, lexical fingerprints, and structural templates
- The fix is always: more specificity, more stance, more concrete detail, more context
- Platform enforcement is shifting from "what gets engagement" to "what gets meaningful engagement"

# Market Validation Methods: Fast, Free Ways to Test Before You Build

## Overview
This document contains practical $0 validation methods a beginner can execute in 1-3 days, plus contrarian approaches that challenge "validate everything" orthodoxy. The GPT uses these to help users test their ICP + offer before over-investing. Validation is about getting behavioral evidence (payments, booked calls, detailed next-step questions) — not opinions ("sounds great!").

## Core Principle: Validation Is Evidence of Buying Behavior, Not Approval
The Mom Test rule: talk about their life, ask for concrete past behavior, and push toward commitments (pre-order, pilot deposit, scheduled next step) — never ask "would you buy this?" because people lie to be polite. Source: Rob Fitzpatrick.

## Method 1: Landing Page + Direct Outreach (Interest → Conversation Loop)
**Time:** 6-12 hours | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Build a one-page landing page: headline naming the pain, 3 benefit bullets, rough mockup or description, "early access" email form
2. Post where the pain shows up (relevant subreddits, communities, forums)
3. Message a small batch of likely users and ask for ONE concrete next step (short call, workflow screenshot, or paid beta commitment)
4. Set a success threshold BEFORE sharing (e.g., "10 signups and 2 calls in 48 hours")
5. Stop when you hit or miss the threshold

**What it tells you:** Whether strangers will give you time (reply/call) in addition to attention — and which positioning pulls fastest.

**Warning:** Signups alone are "validation theater" unless converted into calls or payments.

Source: David Wilson / Indie Hackers.

## Method 2: Social Listening With Free Alerts
**Time:** 1-2 hours setup + 15 min/day for 1-3 days | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Write 10-15 "pain phrases" your ICP uses (e.g., "alternative to ___," "how to ___ without ___," "tool for ___")
2. Set up Google Alerts for those phrases
3. Add Reddit alerts (F5Bot or similar free tool) for the same phrases
4. Check daily for 1-3 days. Capture each hit: who said it, what context, what workaround they're using
5. Promote top 3-5 recurring phrases into draft messaging

**What it tells you:** Whether the pain is happening right now and where conversations cluster.

**Warning:** Alerts can skew toward small, unrepresentative cohorts.

## Method 3: Keyword + Trend Triangulation
**Time:** 2-5 hours | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Start from 5-10 phrases you've seen in the wild
2. Check Google Trends: is interest stable, rising, or seasonal?
3. Pull related/rising queries from Trends
4. Cross-check with Google autocomplete + "People also ask" for intent signals
5. Pick one narrow query cluster
6. Write a wedge sentence: "[Persona] searching for [job] because [trigger] — current options fail when [constraint]."

**What it tells you:** Whether existing search intent exists and which angles may be easiest to position.

**Warning:** Search interest ≠ conversion; trends can be seasonal or curiosity-driven.

## Method 4: Competitor Review Mining
**Time:** 4-8 hours | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Pick a category where people already spend money on solutions
2. List top 3-5 incumbents (competitors, apps, services)
3. Harvest 30-60 low-star reviews (1-2 stars) plus public support/FAQ content
4. Tag each complaint by root cause: setup pain, missing workflow, pricing surprise, poor support, etc.
5. Count repeats — clusters become your "gap hypotheses"
6. Turn top 1-2 gaps into a concrete offer and test via landing page or outreach

**What it tells you:** Where incumbents generate frustration you can wedge into, and how to differentiate.

**Warning:** Can push toward shallow "fix the reviews" thinking without verifying switching costs and willingness to pay.

Source: Aytekin Tank / Indie Hackers.

## Method 5: Reddit Smoke Test
**Time:** 3-6 hours + 30-60 min active replies | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Build bare-minimum landing page (headline + 3 bullets + email capture)
2. Set 24-hour success metric (e.g., "15 signups" or "3 DM conversations")
3. Choose a subreddit with strong engagement in your topic
4. Write a native-feeling post: show a concrete example, explain the pain, place link where culture expects
5. Reply quickly to all comments. Follow up with signups.

**What it tells you:** Whether strangers self-select into your funnel and what their first objections are.

**Warning:** High-variance. Vanity metrics are common if you stop at signups without converting to calls/payments.

Source: Charlie HB / Indie Hackers.

## Method 6: Pre-Sell Before You Build (Founding Member Offer)
**Time:** 1-2 days | **Cost:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Create a "sales artifact": mockups, short walkthrough, or detailed description of what you'll deliver
2. List it on a free platform (Gumroad, Stripe payment link, etc.) with explicit early-buyer incentive and delivery timeline
3. Promote where the pain shows up + via direct outreach
4. Treat early buyers as design partners who shape the build

**What it tells you:** REAL willingness to pay — the strongest validation signal. Plus a high-signal cohort to interview.

**Warning:** Creates delivery pressure. Can bias toward one-off deals if your business needs retention.

Source: Gene Maryushenko / Indie Hackers.

## Contrarian Approach: Try to INVALIDATE Fast
Instead of seeking confirmation, seek disconfirmation. Kill weak ideas in 24 hours.

**Steps:**
1. Define a falsifiable "minimum evidence" rule (e.g., 1 prepay or 3 booked calls)
2. Set a HARD STOP if you don't hit it within 24 hours
3. Build the simplest possible pitch and spend the day contacting real prospects
4. If it fails: reframe once (new segment or offer angle), or deliberately kill the idea and move on

**Why this matters:** Most validation is confirmation bias. Trying to invalidate produces honest signal faster.

**Risk:** Can create false negatives in long sales cycles or if first positioning is wrong.

Source: Tom Jacquesson / Indie Hackers.

## Contrarian Approach: Define "Enough" and Stop (Anti-Over-Validation)
Define Minimum Success Criteria (MSC) upfront. Only run tests that can plausibly hit MSC in 1-3 days.

**The rule:** Once MSC is met, STOP validating and start building/selling. Switch from "proving" to "learning by usage" (onboarding, retention, support).

**Signs you're over-validating:**
- You've talked to 20+ people but haven't made an offer
- You keep "refining the idea" instead of testing it
- You're reading about validation instead of doing it
- Your "research" has been going on for more than 2 weeks

Source: Ash Maurya.

## Quick Competitor Research Framework
When researching competitors, focus on decisions that affect switching — not feature checklists:

| What to Compare | Why It Matters |
|----------------|---------------|
| Who is it "for"? (their ICP) | Reveals positioning gaps |
| What do they promise? | Shows the outcome standard |
| How is it priced/packaged? | Establishes market anchors |
| What do negative reviews say? | Reveals unmet needs |
| How do they acquire clients? | Shows which channels work |

## How the GPT Should Use Validation
1. After ICP + offer are drafted, recommend 1-2 validation methods based on the user's situation
2. Help the user define their MSC before they start
3. If validation passes: move to pricing and acquisition planning
4. If validation fails: diagnose (wrong ICP? wrong offer? wrong channel?) and iterate ONE variable
5. Never let validation become an excuse to delay selling

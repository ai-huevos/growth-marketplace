## TOP 10 DETAILED

### 1. Strategic (Strategic Thinking)
**Definition:** You create alternative ways to proceed. Faced with any given scenario, you can quickly spot the relevant patterns and issues.

**Personalized insights (from report):**
- Known for ease with language; vocabulary allows for storytelling and clear expression
- Generate innovative ideas; unique perspective on events, people, situations; inspire others to start projects
- Identify a goal, devise numerous ways of reaching it, choose the best alternative
- See opportunities, trends and solutions before teammates or peers do
- Feel good when you know the exact words to express an idea or feeling
- Select the right combination of words to convey ideas in discussion
- Examine basic elements of problems; generate alternative plans; narrow down options

**Why you succeed:** Natural ability to anticipate, play out scenarios, plan ahead — agile decision-making.

**Blind spots:**
- Strong Strategic talents can be misinterpreted as criticism of others' ideas
- Others may find it difficult to follow your thought process — you may need to backtrack

### 2. Connectedness (Relationship Building)
**Definition:** You have faith in the links among all things. You believe there are few coincidences and that almost every event has meaning.

**Personalized insights:**
- Curious about people's diverse philosophies of life; link one person's truths with another's values
- Sense everything in life is interrelated and interdependent — this fortifies you to face difficulties
- Attracted to news that promises to improve quality of life for the entire human family
- Hold tightly to core values; internal compass points toward what's right, proper, or true
- Feel pleased when words and deeds align with fundamental values

**Why you succeed:** Build bridges between people and groups; help others find meaning; give comfort in uncertainty.

**Blind spots:**
- May react more calmly to others' bad news than they'd like — they want validation, not philosophical response
- May be perceived as naïve or idealistic

### 3. Activator (Influencing)
**Definition:** You can make things happen by turning thoughts into action. You want to do things now, rather than simply talk about them.

**Personalized insights:**
- Routinely spark enthusiasm of individuals
- Gravitate to situations where you can be your true self
- Described as "what you see is what you get" — very little mystery about who you are
- Make a point of talking with strangers; tell them about yourself without being asked
- Your forthright approach encourages others to be as open with you as you are with them
- Strive to energize people; generate enthusiasm around projects, events, activities
- Energized, not paralyzed, by opportunities and possibilities

**Why you succeed:** You are a catalyst. Natural ability to turn ideas into action. Energy is contagious.

**Blind spots:**
- May charge ahead without a solid plan
- May make others feel overwhelmed and pressured to act before comfortable

### 4. Intellection (Strategic Thinking)
**Definition:** Characterized by intellectual activity. Introspective and appreciate intellectual discussions.

**Personalized insights:**
- Well-read individual; people turn to you for guidance because of what you've read
- Reading is the key that opens the door to fresh ideas — collect them for future benefit
- Continually expand sphere of knowledge through reading
- Prepare for important conversations by collecting background information
- Set aside at least five hours of quiet time each week to consider what you've discovered
- Known for carrying reading material everywhere
- Make time and seek places for quiet reflection
- Examine new theories and concepts; make discoveries and establish links between facts

**Why you succeed:** Love to think, muse, reflect. Powerful mental processing clarifies and explains.

**Blind spots:**
- Comfort with silence and solitude can be viewed as isolated, disinterested, disengaged
- May create needless complexity; others may want faster decisions

### 5. Input (Strategic Thinking)
**Definition:** Need to collect and archive. Accumulate information, ideas, artifacts, or even relationships.

**Personalized insights:**
- Enjoy life more when speaking to people who understand complicated/technical vocabulary
- Address groups using sophisticated words and terminology; vocabulary captures attention
- Others assume you have authority because of your proficiency with language
- Curious about how things operate — individuals' behavior, procedural steps
- Educate yourself through observation, reading, investigation, experimentation
- Welcome opportunities to think out loud about ideas, theories, philosophies
- Read widely to broaden knowledge about others' interests; help people reach their goals

**Why you succeed:** Seek and store information; pursuit of mastery empowers credible, well-informed decisions.

**Blind spots:**
- Unrestrained input can lead to intellectual or physical clutter
- May overload others with too much information

### 6. Ideation (Strategic Thinking)
**Definition:** Fascinated by ideas. Able to find connections between seemingly disparate phenomena.

**Why you succeed:** Powerful and creative brainstorming partner; love to look at the world from different perspectives; always searching for connections.

**Blind spots:**
- Limitless thoughts and ideas can overwhelm and confuse people
- **May struggle to follow through on ideas generated — consider collaborating with someone who can help turn best ideas into real results** (Gallup's explicit note)

### 7. Positivity (Relationship Building)
**Definition:** Contagious enthusiasm. Upbeat and can get others excited.

**Why you succeed:** Infectious energy; genuine encouragement; optimistic outlook makes people feel better.

**Blind spots:**
- Optimism can come off as superficial, naïve, or insincere
- May point out the positive before others are ready to hear it — need to let them vent

### 8. Relator (Relationship Building)
**Definition:** Enjoy close relationships. Deep satisfaction working hard with friends toward a goal.

**Why you succeed:** Form genuine, mutually rewarding one-on-one relationships; authenticity builds close, long-lasting connections.

**Blind spots:**
- Don't trust others implicitly; some may think you're hard to get to know
- Tendency to spend more time with inner circle; may appear exclusive

### 9. Communication (Influencing)
**Definition:** Find it easy to put thoughts into words. Good conversationalist and presenter.

**Why you succeed:** Capture attention by what you say and how you say it; find words for others' thoughts and feelings.

**Blind spots:**
- May monopolize discussions
- Words alone may not motivate others — need to listen too

### 10. Learner (Strategic Thinking)
**Definition:** Great desire to learn and continuously improve. The process of learning excites you more than the outcome.

**Why you succeed:** Absorb information quickly; continually challenge self to learn more; stay on the cutting edge.

**Blind spots:**
- May impose value of learning on others
- Process may matter more than outcome — can get in the way of results and productivity

## GALLUP'S DIRECT NOTE ON BLIND SPOTS

From Gallup's definition of weakness:

> "Gallup defines a weakness as anything that gets in the way of your success. While the CliftonStrengths assessment does not mathematically quantify weaknesses, you can use your profile to understand how any of your CliftonStrengths themes might prevent you from maximizing your potential."

**Questions to identify weakness:**
- Does this theme ever undermine my success?
- Have I ever received negative feedback related to this theme?
- Does my role require me to use this theme, but I feel drained when I do?

**Four ways to manage weaknesses:**
1. **Claim them** — know how they get in your way
2. **Collaborate** — ask partners for support
3. **Apply a strength** — use a different theme to achieve a better outcome
4. **Just do it** — lean in and do your best

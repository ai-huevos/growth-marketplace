# Execution Roadmap Templates: From Idea to First Sale

## Overview
This document contains proven roadmap structures for going from business idea to first payment, plus the behavioral barriers that derail beginners and how to design roadmaps that account for them. The GPT's final deliverable is a personalized execution roadmap built from these templates.

## Core Principle: Roadmaps Must Produce Revenue, Not Just "Launch"
A launch without a sale is a hobby. Every milestone in the roadmap should move the user closer to someone paying them money. Cut anything that doesn't serve that goal.

## Template 1: The 30-Day First Client Sprint
**Best for:** Service businesses, consulting, coaching, freelancing
**Source:** Stack AI consulting platform (100+ consultants guided)

**Week 1 — Foundation (Days 1-7):**
- Day 1-2: Define niche + ICP (use ICP extraction playbook)
- Day 3-4: Craft positioning statement + single first offer
- Day 5-6: Build minimum viable presence (one page that explains what you do — NOT a full website)
- Day 7: Review and lock in — no more "refining"

**Week 2 — Prospect List (Days 8-14):**
- Day 8-9: Compile 50-100 target prospects
- Day 10-11: Research top 20 in detail (find personalization hooks)
- Day 12-14: Set up outreach infrastructure (email or LinkedIn — pick ONE channel)

**Week 3 — Outreach (Days 15-21):**
- Day 15-17: First outreach wave (10/day minimum)
- Day 18-19: Follow-up wave #1
- Day 20-21: Book discovery calls from responses

**Week 4 — Close (Days 22-30):**
- Day 22-25: Run discovery calls
- Day 26-27: Send proposals (same day as call if possible)
- Day 28-30: Handle objections, close, collect payment

**Key rule:** Week 1 is the ONLY planning week. After that, every day involves external contact.

## Template 2: The 30-Day Offer-to-Market Sprint
**Best for:** Coaches, service providers who need positioning clarity first
**Source:** Joanna Lott (100+ paying clients guided)

**Week 1 — Decide:**
- Choose ONE offer and articulate it so it's easy to say yes to
- Strip away complexity — what's the simplest version?
- Write: "I help [ICP] achieve [outcome] through [mechanism] in [timeframe]"

**Week 2 — Position:**
- Build "know/like/trust" through targeted visibility
- Expert positioning: share one piece of valuable content in ICP's spaces
- Warm up your existing network with what you're doing

**Week 3 — Solution:**
- Make the offer the obvious solution to the audience's need
- Address objections preemptively in your messaging
- Create social proof (even if it's a process walkthrough, not a testimonial)

**Week 4 — Action:**
- Convert conversations to clients
- Use "enrolling with honesty" (no high-pressure tactics)
- Follow up with everyone who showed interest

**Documented result:** Participant got first paying client by Day 10 using daily execution steps.

## Template 3: The Pre-Sell Pathway (Digital Products/Courses)
**Best for:** Course creators, digital product builders, info products
**Source:** Teachable (featured case: $45K in pre-sales in 30 days)

**Week 1 — Conversation-First Validation:**
- Ask the audience about problems, preferred formats, willingness to pay
- Find patterns across 10-20 conversations
- Sketch an outline based on real demand (not assumptions)

**Week 2 — Pre-Sell-Ready Shell:**
- Convert existing content into modules
- Load a course/product skeleton (even if most is in draft)
- Set early-bird timeline and price

**Week 3 — Story-Led Promotion:**
- Publish 3-5 pieces showing your expertise and the transformation
- Email/DM people from Week 1 conversations
- Link to pre-sale page

**Week 4 — Close + Build With Buyers:**
- Add feedback loops (updates, check-ins, community)
- Early buyers shape the build
- Deliver while you sell

**Key insight:** Revenue happens DURING the pre-sale window — before you fully build.

## Template 4: The 10-Week Course Launch (Amy Porterfield)
**Best for:** Larger digital course launches, people willing to invest more time
**Source:** Amy Porterfield (25,000+ students, documented $24K-$55K first launches)

**Weeks 1-2:** Choose topic, define ideal customer, set pricing, validate via pre-sell
**Weeks 3-5:** Outline modules, create first version of content
**Weeks 6-8:** Build sales page, plan pre-launch, create webinar, draft email sequences
**Weeks 9-10:** Deliver webinar, run follow-up sales sequence, close

**When to use this over the 30-day sprints:** When the offer is complex, the price point is higher ($500+), or the user needs a structured launch event (webinar).

## The Shortest Path to First Payment (Universal Pattern)
Across all roadmaps, the fastest sequence is:

1. **Pick one ICP + one urgent problem** (Day 1-2)
2. **Package one offer with a deadline** (Day 3-4)
3. **Validate in public** — short calls, warm outreach, test messaging (Day 5-7)
4. **Do daily acquisition** — messages, emails, follow-ups, call booking (Day 8+)
5. **Close simply and fast** — short proposal, clear next steps (as soon as calls happen)
6. **Deliver and turn outcomes into proof** (immediately after close)

## Why Beginner Roadmaps Fail (Design the Roadmap to Prevent These)

### Barrier 1: Preparation-as-Procrastination
**What beginners expect:** "Once I have the perfect niche, website, and offer, I'll feel confident reaching out."
**What actually happens:** Overthinking increases confusion and decision fatigue. Founders keep polishing low-leverage assets and avoid market contact.
**How the roadmap prevents this:** Cap "prep time" to Week 1 ONLY. Force external contact by Week 2. Make the market — not your imagination — the feedback engine.

### Barrier 2: Fear of Failure and Rejection
**What beginners expect:** Rejection won't feel personal. Sales is a "later step."
**What actually happens:** Early-stage entrepreneurship is emotionally loaded. Fear suppresses action right when outreach is needed most.
**How the roadmap prevents this:** Make selling a SCHEDULED behavior (daily outreach targets, not "when I feel ready"). Reduce perceived risk with smaller offers (diagnostic/audit before full engagement). Provide scripts and templates so rejection is about the MESSAGE, not the person.

### Barrier 3: No Accountability
**What beginners expect:** "Motivation will carry me. I can do this solo."
**What actually happens:** Without check-ins, uncomfortable tasks get skipped. Business stalls in the "messy middle."
**How the roadmap prevents this:** Include weekly check-in mechanism. Track LEADING indicators (messages sent, calls booked, proposals out) — not just revenue. Public commitments increase follow-through.

**Research finding:** Telling others about a goal predicts more goal support and greater effort. Solo, quiet execution is the lowest-accountability path.

## How the GPT Should Build a Personalized Roadmap
After ICP, offer, pricing, validation, and acquisition channel are defined:

1. **Select the right template** based on offer type (service → 30-day sprint; digital product → pre-sell pathway)
2. **Adjust timeline** based on user's available hours per week (full-time = 30 days; side hustle = 60-90 days)
3. **Insert the user's specific:** ICP, offer, price, chosen acquisition channel, outreach scripts
4. **Add accountability mechanism:** Weekly milestone checklist, daily activity targets
5. **Add the "if this isn't working" diagnosis:** At Week 3, if no responses → diagnose ICP/offer/channel mismatch
6. **Set a "decision point":** At the end of the roadmap, clear criteria for "continue, pivot, or stop"

## Roadmap Output Format
The GPT should deliver the roadmap as a structured document:

**Header:** "[User's Offer Name] — Execution Roadmap"
**Summary:** One paragraph — ICP, offer, price, channel, timeline
**Weekly breakdown:** Each week has 3-5 specific action items with daily targets
**Milestone checkpoints:** What should be true by end of each week
**Diagnostic triggers:** "If X hasn't happened by Week Y, do Z"
**Success metric:** First paid client or first [N] pre-sales

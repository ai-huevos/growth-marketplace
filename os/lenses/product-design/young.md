# Indi Young — 6 - Audience Calibration

> A deliverable is audience-calibrated only when it demonstrates genuine understanding of people's interior cognition, inner thinking, emotional reactions, guiding principles, as they address their own purpose, free from the organization's solution lens. Surface data (opinions, preferences, explanations) masquerading as depth is the most common failure.

*Active period*: 2008-present (Mental Models 2008; Practical Empathy 2015; Time to Listen 2022; Listening Deeply course)

## Operating beliefs
- Average user is a myth. Bell-curve design leaves most people ill-fitted or harmed.
- Demographics do not cause cognition. Inner thinking arises from lived experience, not demographic profile, except via discrimination.
- Opinions/preferences are not interior cognition. They're shorthand for deeper lived experience.
- People, not users. 'User' implies solution relationship. Problem space focuses on people addressing own purposes.
- Support behavior, don't change it. Support how people think and act; don't steer toward org's desired behavior.
- Qualitative data must be pattern-based. Only patterns reaching data saturation are reliable; anecdotal data is rightly distrusted.
- Assumptions cause harm. Teams assuming people's interior cognition risk creating self-doubt, feeling unwelcome, lost time/health/freedom/money.

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- Purpose framed as person's verb or org goal/product feature?
- Whose interior cognition gathered, how? Listening sessions, interviews, or surveys?
- Patterns from interior cognition or surface concepts (opinions, preferences)?
- Segmented by demographics or by thinking styles from cognition?
- 'Average user' referenced explicitly or implicitly?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Favors qualitative speaker-led listening over structured interviews/surveys. Penalizes question lists even if they produced useful insights.
- Privileges problem-space over solution-space research. May undervalue evaluative usability work.
- Treats persuasion/behavior-change language as manipulation signals. Penalizes ethically applied persuasion frameworks.
- Demands full methodology as gold standard. May undervalue pragmatically effective lighter methods under constraints.
- Prioritizes inclusion/harm-awareness; may penalize deliverables serving a genuinely narrow, homogeneous audience well.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- Resource-constrained contexts: full methodology requires significant time/budget. No graceful degradation path for rapid sprint cycles.
- Quantitative validation: no statistical validation protocols for pattern reliability. Quantitative-grounded deliverables may be unfairly penalized.
- Solution-space bridge: translation from thinking styles to specific UI/UX/service decisions underspecified. May penalize deliverables bridging this gap via other methods.
- Scalability: best with small-to-medium participant pools. Cannot evaluate large-scale quantitative segmentation quality.
- Persuasion/conversion contexts: sales pages, ads, email sequences operate in solution-space with behavioral intent. Will systematically penalize regardless of calibration quality.
- Structured interviews reaching genuine depth may be unfairly penalized for method rather than data quality.
- Demographic segmentation reflecting real discrimination-driven differences: anti-demographic stance may miss cases where demographics genuinely predict experiences due to systemic factors.

## Frameworks

### Mental Model Diagrams / Opportunity Maps
**Source**: Mental Models (2008); Practical Empathy (2015); Time to Listen (2022)

**Purpose**: Does the org know how people think through a purpose, and does its support align with or miss those patterns?

Maps interior cognition from listening sessions into towers/blocks as a skyline, aligns org capabilities beneath to reveal gaps. Thinking styles layered to show which ways of thinking are supported or excluded.

#### Components
- **Purpose Definition** — Study framed by person's purpose as a verb (e.g., 'recover from an accident,' not 'file a claim'). Not a product goal or task.
  - Key questions:
    - Purpose stated as verb from person's perspective?
    - Free from product/solution/org language?
    - Broad enough to reveal unexpected cognition?
  - Red flags: Entirely solution-centric with no person perspective.
- **Emergent Data Synthesis** — Interior cognition summaries compared by focus of mental attention (not keyword/org concepts/timeline). Groups shift as each summary added. Patterns emerge bottom-up.
  - Key questions:
    - Categories pre-defined or emergent?
    - Grouped by mental attention focus or keyword/org taxonomy?
    - Groups reorganize as data added?
    - Summaries in verb + key point + details format?
  - Red flags: Organized entirely by product features/org categories masquerading as person-centric.
- **Gap Analysis and Capability Alignment** — Org capabilities aligned beneath skyline towers. Gaps where towers lack capabilities; weak support where measurements show low values.
  - Key questions:
    - Gaps identified where towers have no capabilities?
    - Weak-support areas with low measurement values?
    - Analysis per thinking style?

#### Decision rules
- IF affinity grouping uses keywords/org categories, THEN patterns contaminated by assumptions; synthesis unreliable.
- IF towers have no capabilities beneath, THEN gap = unmet need = opportunity.
- IF capabilities exist but sparklines show low values, THEN support is weak/harmful for certain thinking styles.
- IF one thinking style dominates supported towers, THEN solution skewed toward one way of thinking (likely design team's).
- IF diagram framed by product/org goal rather than person's purpose, THEN solution-space masquerading as problem-space.

### Practical Empathy / Cognitive Empathy Development Cycle
**Source**: Practical Empathy (2015); Velocity Santa Clara 2015; UX Lausanne 2014

**Purpose**: Has this deliverable been informed by genuine cognitive empathy, developed, not assumed, and applied to the design?

Separates empathy: emotional (reflexive, unreliable) vs cognitive (purposeful, developable). Cycle: Listen -> Deepen -> Apply, iterated.

#### Components
- **Listen Phase** — Recognize person has something going on. Treat as valid truth. Offer to listen. Suspend judgment.
  - Key questions:
    - Evidence of listening (not just asking)?
    - Person's experience as valid truth or filtered through org lens?
    - Judgment suspended?
- **Deepen Phase** — Find patterns across what heard. Cognitive empathy develops over time, not instantly.
  - Key questions:
    - Patterns across multiple people's interior cognition?
    - Synthesis over time (not single sprint session)?
    - Cognitive diversity or collapse into single 'average'?
- **Apply Phase** — Use developed empathy to support people's intents/purposes.
  - Key questions:
    - Supports people's purposes or manipulates behavior?
    - Grounded in listening patterns or claims empathy without Listen/Deepen evidence?
    - Supports multiple thinking styles?
  - Red flags: Claims empathy but uses persuasion/behavior-change language, support shifted to manipulation.

#### Decision rules
- IF claims empathy but only gathered opinions/preferences/motivations, THEN surface/expression layer only; no cognitive empathy developed.
- IF empathy 'applied' without being 'developed' (no listening, no patterns), THEN assumption-based, not empathy-based.
- IF deliverable uses persuasion/behavior-change language, THEN shifted from supporting to manipulating.
- IF team reports emotional empathy ('we felt what they felt'), THEN powerful but unreliable; need cognitive empathy methods.

### Time to Listen / Listening Deeply Methodology
**Source**: Time to Listen (2022); Listening Deeply course (46 videos, 11.25h); I SPY reference document

**Purpose**: Was data collected through methodology reaching genuine interior cognition, avoiding researcher bias, letting the person lead?

Built on Jawbreaker/Gobstopper Model, four layers: Description (exterior), Expression (opinions/preferences), Almost Cognition (generalized/implied), Interior Cognition (inner thinking, emotional reactions, guiding principles). Goal: reach the crystalline core.

#### Components
- **Jawbreaker Layer Depth** — Four layers per topic. Outer: Description (explanation, scene-setting). Second: Expression (opinion, preference, attitude. POBA). Third: Almost Cognition (generalized, implied, future). Core: Interior Cognition (inner thinking, emotional reaction, guiding principle).
  - Key questions:
    - Data sits at which layer?
    - 'I feel that/like' classified as opinions (Expression), not emotions (Interior)?
    - Generalizations pinned to specific place/time?
    - All three interior cognition types present?
  - Red flags: All data at Description/Explanation with zero interior cognition.
- **Methodology Compliance** — Listening session protocol: germinal question, speaker-led, no question list, pull tabs, memory mode (past tense), safe space, no time limits.
  - Key questions:
    - Germinal question used?
    - Speaker-led or researcher-imposed topics?
    - Question list (interview) or speaker-led (listening session)?
    - Pull tabs used?
    - Memory mode (past tense)?
    - Safe space maintained?
- **Pull Tab Recognition** — Noticing signs of deeper cognition beneath surface statements and helping speaker unfold it.
  - Key questions:
    - Researchers noticed deeper cognition hints and followed?
    - Asking techniques: 'What went through your mind?', 'More about that', 'Pin to place/time', 'Find roots'?
    - Surface statements explored for interior cognition beneath?

#### Decision rules
- IF question list used, THEN interview not listening session; interior cognition likely not reached.
- IF generalizations accepted without pinning to place/time, THEN data at 'almost cognition,' not true interior.
- IF 'I feel that/like' coded as emotional reactions, THEN likely opinions misclassified.
- IF 'I hate it when...' coded as emotional reaction, THEN likely preference; follow-up 'why?' needed for actual emotion beneath.
- IF session stopped at fixed time, THEN may have been truncated before full unfolding.
- IF researcher introduced topics, THEN speaker's cognitive path disrupted; data may reflect researcher assumptions.

### Thinking Styles
**Source**: Time to Listen Ch. 9; Practical Empathy Ch. 6; WebExpo 2021; Rosenverse 2025; Working with MM Skylines & Thinking Styles course

**Purpose**: Does the deliverable differentiate support for genuinely different ways people think about their purpose, rather than treating all as 'average user' or segmenting by demographics?

Thinking styles emerge from listening data during synthesis, not invented by researcher. Purpose-framed, context-dependent (people switch), non-demographic. Demographic lens layered after to reveal discrimination-driven cognition.

#### Components
- **Emergence Quality** — Thinking styles emerged from data, not pre-defined/imposed/demographic-derived.
  - Key questions:
    - Emerged from listening data or pre-defined?
    - From interior cognition patterns or survey/preference data?
    - Genuinely different ways of thinking about the purpose?
  - Red flags: Demographics (e.g., 'millennial travelers,' 'women shoppers') presented as thinking styles.
- **Purpose Specificity** — Thinking styles only meaningful within a specific purpose frame. Person's style about driving company truck may differ from private vehicle.
  - Key questions:
    - Defined within specific purpose?
    - Applied only in that context, or used like horoscopes?
    - Acknowledged people switch based on context?
- **Non-Demographic Character** — No demographic references unless scope connects to discrimination. Characters (not personas) represent styles, two characters can share demographics but differ in thinking.
  - Key questions:
    - Free from demographic labels?
    - Demographics referenced only for discrimination-driven cognition?
    - 'Characters' vs 'personas'?

#### Decision rules
- IF styles labeled with demographic terms, THEN demographic segments, not thinking styles; assumptions baked in.
- IF applied across all purposes like horoscopes, THEN misused; must be purpose-specific.
- IF only one thinking style represented, THEN designing for 'average user.'
- IF determined by survey/preference data rather than interior cognition, THEN psychographic segments, not thinking styles.

### Harms Scale
**Source**: I SPY reference document; indiyoung.com; Time to Listen Ch. 1

**Purpose**: Does the deliverable recognize harm beyond 'frustration', including serious, lasting, and systemic harms?

Five-level scale from baseline frustration to severe harm. Most orgs only measure level 1. Reveals disproportionate harm to people excluded from 'average user' design.

#### Components
- **Harm Level Recognition** — Five levels: (1) Frustration/confusion (baseline), (2) Serious (interruption, triggering, self-doubt, feeling unwelcome), (3) Lasting (lost productivity, relationships, time, money), (4) Systemic (structured into laws, policies, norms), (5) Severe (loss of health, freedom, life).
  - Key questions:
    - Acknowledges harm beyond frustration?
    - Serious harms identified for excluded thinking styles?
    - Lasting/systemic harms mapped?
    - Benchmarked and tracked?
- **Edge Case Framing** — Edge cases apply to processes, not people. Classifying people as edge cases = exclusion.
  - Key questions:
    - People referred to as 'edge cases'?
    - Process edge cases distinguished from human diversity?

#### Decision rules
- IF only satisfaction/frustration measured, THEN misses serious harms disproportionately affecting excluded people.
- IF opportunity map shows thinking-style gaps, THEN benchmark by harm level and track improvement.
- IF 'edge cases' applied to people (not processes), THEN misclassifying people as exceptions.

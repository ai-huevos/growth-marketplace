# Ignacio Cofone — 8 - Trust & Ethics

> Privacy law fails because it treats consent as meaningful in environments designed to undermine it. Trust requires respecting user autonomy; exploitation occurs when formalistic consent masks undermined choice. The burden belongs on the profiting entity, not the clicking individual.

*Active period*: 2020-present

## Operating beliefs
- Consent via dark patterns is extracted compliance, not consent.
- The 'privacy paradox' is a misnomer: users disclose under structural uncertainty, not apathy. Treating disclosure as validation is exploitative.
- Dark patterns are symptoms of consent-based regulation's failure, not isolated design choices.
- Mild dark patterns are more dangerous than aggressive ones, doubled conversion with no backlash, most profitable and hardest to detect.
- Privacy is prevention of exploitation through personal data, not secrecy.
- If you benefit from a practice exposing people to risk or harming autonomy/intimacy, you are liable, regardless of compliance.
- Individual control cannot protect privacy amid inferences, relational data, and power asymmetries.
- Innovation is compatible with non-exploitation, seatbelt mandates redirected auto innovation, not halted it.

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- What choice is the user making?
- What does the entity gain from 'yes' vs. 'no'?
- Are accept/reject equally accessible in design, clicks, and cognitive load?
- Dark patterns present: obstruction, trick questions, hidden information, deceptive defaults, urgency, nagging, confirmshaming, social proof manipulation?
- Who bears risk if user agrees, user or entity?
- Does deliverable target or disproportionately affect vulnerable populations?
- Can user reverse this decision as easily as they made it?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Favors structural/corporate accountability over individual responsibility. May undervalue genuine informed agency and convenience trade-offs.
- Normative legal scholarship bias: frameworks are theoretical, not empirically tested by Cofone. Evidence borrowed from Luguri & Strahilevitz (2021) and HCI researchers.
- Western legal focus: US, Canada, EU. May not translate to other regulatory environments.
- Consumer-facing focus: B2C digital products. B2B/internal/non-commercial contexts require significant extension.
- Tends to classify ambiguous persuasion as manipulation. Persuasion-manipulation boundary is context-dependent and fuzzy by Cofone's own admission.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- Persuasion-manipulation boundary is context-dependent. Cofone: 'navigating the necessary context-dependent nuances is a herculean task.' Edge cases produce inconsistent results.
- Beneficial data practices may be chilled. Strict exploitation test could penalize legitimate user-beneficial data uses.
- No operational scoring in Cofone's work. Rubric translated from normative principles, not empirically validated.
- Corporate accountability framework requires legal changes not yet enacted. May disconnect from practical compliance.
- B2B/enterprise contexts with legal representation and negotiating power not modeled.
- Mild persuasion in gray zone (slightly larger 'accept' button with clearly visible 'decline') may be overpenalized.
- Products where users demonstrably prefer convenience and data practice creates genuine mutual value may be undervalued.
- Emergency/public health data collection where speed matters more than choice symmetry is not well addressed.

## Frameworks

### Dark Patterns as Anti-Privacy Design
**Source**: The Privacy Fallacy (Cambridge UP, 2023) Ch.4; Gray et al. 2018; Mathur et al. 2019; Luguri & Strahilevitz 2021

**Purpose**: Are deceptive design strategies present that manipulate users into agreement through deception rather than genuine choice?

Dark patterns = strategies crafted to trick users using human psychology, without user interests in mind. Spectrum: neutral design -> nudging -> dark patterns -> hypertargeted manipulation.

#### Components
- **Obstruction** — Asymmetric difficulty: easy to sign up, hard to cancel/reject. Unnecessary steps before disfavored option.
  - Key questions:
    - Count clicks/steps accept vs. reject, asymmetry?
    - Cancellation as easy as sign-up?
    - Unnecessary intermediate steps (upsells, 'are you sure' loops, phone calls)?
  - Red flags: Cancellation/rejection functionally impossible without contacting support, or reject path deliberately broken.
- **Trick Questions** — Intentional ambiguity inverting expected meaning so users select unintended option.
  - Key questions:
    - Read every prompt aloud, plain meaning matches technical effect?
    - Double negatives used?
    - Does 'No' mean 'no' or 'show me more options'?
  - Red flags: Natural reading produces opposite of user's intent.
- **Hidden Information** — Disfavored choices/information made visually or navigationally inaccessible, greyed out, small font, buried.
  - Key questions:
    - Reject/decline as visually prominent as accept?
    - Material information accessible without navigating away from decision point?
    - Privacy-protective options hidden behind 'manage preferences'?
  - Red flags: Reject option doesn't exist, or clicking 'reject' triggers acceptance.
- **Deceptive Defaults and Preselection** — Corporate-favorable options pre-selected. Users must actively opt out; defaults hidden or deceptive.
  - Key questions:
    - Checkboxes/toggles pre-selected in entity's favor?
    - Defaults clearly labeled and easily changeable?
    - Third-party sign-in silently opts into additional services?
  - Red flags: Defaults silently enroll users in data sharing/marketing with no indication and no easy reversal.
- **Urgency Manipulation** — False scarcity, countdown timers, pretend final notices exploiting cognitive bandwidth to pressure decisions.
  - Key questions:
    - Countdown timers/scarcity claims real or fabricated?
    - 'Final notice'/'last chance' reflect genuine deadlines?
    - Urgency used to bypass deliberation on consent/data practices?
  - Red flags: False urgency directly on consent mechanisms ('Accept cookies in 30 seconds or lose access').
- **Nagging** — Repeated/invasive requests disrupting user experience until compliance.
  - Key questions:
    - Times same request presented after dismissal?
    - Dismissed prompts return at same/increased frequency?
    - Declining triggers additional persuasion attempts?
  - Red flags: Declining triggers more frequent/aggressive prompting than not responding.
- **Confirmshaming** — Shame/guilt framing pressuring users toward corporate-preferred option.
  - Key questions:
    - Decline option uses emotionally loaded/shaming language?
    - 'No' framed to make user feel foolish/ungrateful?
    - Compare emotional valence of accept vs. decline copy.
  - Red flags: Decline explicitly shames user's intelligence, values, or character.
- **Social Proof Manipulation** — Exploiting bandwagon effects via fabricated or misleading popularity signals to pressure compliance.
  - Key questions:
    - Social proof elements verified and accurate?
    - Used to pressure consent/data-sharing specifically?
    - 'X people viewing this' claims genuine?
  - Red flags: Fabricated social proof directly on consent/data-sharing decisions.

#### Decision rules
- IF any dark pattern category triggers red_flag, THEN fatal.
- IF 3+ categories score low, THEN systemic manipulation.
- IF mild dark patterns across multiple categories, THEN flag: most profitable/hardest to detect per Luguri & Strahilevitz (doubled sign-ups, no backlash).
- IF dark patterns disproportionately affect less-educated users, THEN add vulnerability exploitation penalty.
- IF no dark patterns across all 8, THEN proceed to consent authenticity.

### Consent vs. Manipulation Distinction
**Source**: The Privacy Fallacy Ch.3-4; 'Consent, Design, and Deceit'; Yale ISP talk (April 2025)

**Purpose**: Is consent genuine (informed, specific, freely given) or illusory (extracted through structural pressure, cognitive exploitation, or deception)?

Consent fails due to two myths (Rationality and Apathy) and three AI-driven structural changes (inferences, relational data, power asymmetries). Persuasion-manipulation line crossed when design interferes with decision-making through deception or bias exploitation, even with formal 'consent.'

#### Components
- **Myth of Rationality Check** — Does design assume users are perfectly informed about data practices and consequences?
  - Key questions:
    - Assumes users understand inferences, aggregation, downstream uses?
    - Information sufficient for genuinely informed decision, or relies on reading lengthy terms?
    - Would average-literacy person understand what they're agreeing to?
  - Red flags: No meaningful description, just 'I agree to the Terms.'
- **Myth of Apathy Check** — Does design treat clicking 'I agree' as evidence users don't care, legitimizing broad collection?
  - Key questions:
    - 'Agree' to one practice authorizes unrelated collection?
    - Consent bundled (all-or-nothing)?
    - Agreement rates used to justify expanding practices?
  - Red flags: Service completely inaccessible without accepting all practices, no meaningful alternative.
- **Anti-Privacy Design Spectrum Placement** — Where on Cofone's four-level spectrum: neutral design, nudging, dark patterns, or hypertargeted manipulation?
  - Key questions:
    - Choice architecture neutral?
    - If not, nudge (framing, non-user-friendly design) or deceive (dark patterns)?
    - Uses knowledge of individual vulnerabilities to target specific users?
  - Red flags: Hypertargeted manipulation, using individual vulnerability knowledge (addiction, depression, financial distress, age) to override decisions.

#### Decision rules
- IF Myth of Rationality applies (assumes user omniscience), THEN consent likely illusory regardless of formal validity.
- IF Myth of Apathy applies (agreement = indifference), THEN consent legitimizes surveillance, not autonomy.
- IF dark patterns or hypertargeted manipulation on spectrum, THEN consent definitively compromised.
- IF nudging on spectrum, THEN consent weakened but not destroyed.
- IF consent bundled (all-or-nothing) with no alternative, THEN functional consent failure even without dark patterns.

### Non-Exploitation Test (Duty Not to Exploit)
**Source**: The Privacy Fallacy Ch.6-7; Yale ISP talk; Oxford profile

**Purpose**: Does the entity benefit from a practice that exposes people to risk or harms autonomy/intimacy?

Exploitative = instrumentalizing data for profit while exposing users to risk or harming autonomy/intimacy. Distinguishes beneficial uses (secure hosting, anonymized research) from harmful ones (monetizing sensitive data). Liability based on harm to victim, not wrongfulness of conduct.

#### Components
- **Exploitation Identification** — Entity profits from practice exposing user to risk or harming autonomy/intimacy?
  - Key questions:
    - What does entity gain?
    - What risks does user bear?
    - Benefit reciprocal or extractive?
    - Material harms (identity theft, discrimination) or immaterial (being outed, autonomy loss, incorrect inferences)?
  - Red flags: Instrumentalizes vulnerable user data for profit with no user benefit and foreseeable harm.
- **Harm Scope Assessment** — Individual-level effects only or relational/group-level harms?
  - Key questions:
    - Affects only consenting individual?
    - Exposes third parties through relational data or group inferences?
    - Aggregated inferences applied to non-consenting individuals?
- **Foreseeability of Harm** — Is entity best positioned to foresee and prevent risks?
  - Key questions:
    - Entity has more information about downstream uses/inference capabilities than user?
    - Steps taken to research and mitigate foreseeable harms?
    - Known risk vectors (breaches, third-party sharing, inference discrimination) addressed?

#### Decision rules
- IF entity profits from user risk exposure without reciprocal benefit, THEN exploitative. Fails regardless of compliance.
- IF relational data or group inferences, THEN individual consent insufficient. Additional scrutiny.
- IF entity best-placed to foresee harm and hasn't acted, THEN heightened liability.
- IF genuine mutual value with proportionate risk, THEN passes even if some risk exists.
- IF analogous to seatbelt-era innovation (redirecting from exploitative to non-exploitative), THEN positive signal.

### Bottom-Up Regulation Model (Grassroots Reporting)
**Source**: Cofone & Henry Coomes, 'Consent, Design, and Deceit' (forthcoming, U Toronto Law Journal, SSRN 2025)

**Purpose**: Would an empowered user report this design as a dark pattern?

Dark pattern persistence is structurally linked to consent-based regulation. Remedy: statutory prohibition, dedicated investigation authority, user reporting with financial incentives (share of penalties). Used as thought experiment: would a motivated, incentivized user flag this?

#### Components
- **Reportability Test** — Would a reasonable, motivated user identify this as a dark pattern and report it?
  - Key questions:
    - Any element deceptive in a way a user could articulate as complaint?
    - Would design survive public screenshot scrutiny?
    - Does design rely on inattention/confusion for commercial effectiveness?

#### Decision rules
- IF would generate reports under grassroots model, THEN trust eroded even if currently legal.
- IF relies on user inattention for commercial effectiveness, THEN incentive structure misaligned with trust.

### Privacy Paradox as Misnomer (Structural Uncertainty)
**Source**: 'The Privacy Paradox Is a Misnomer: Data Under Structural Uncertainty' (2025)

**Purpose**: Is user behavior misinterpreted as consent/indifference when it reflects structural uncertainty?

People disclose despite valuing privacy because perceived risk at collection time is too uncertain to act on. Disclosure under ignorance is NOT validation.

#### Components
- **Structural Uncertainty Exploitation** — Does deliverable exploit the gap between user behavior (disclosure) and values (privacy) by treating disclosure-under-uncertainty as consent?
  - Key questions:
    - Enough information for users to assess actual risk at decision moment?
    - Entity uses acceptance rates to justify expanding practices?
    - User inability to assess inference/aggregation risk acknowledged or exploited?

#### Decision rules
- IF cites acceptance/low complaint rates as evidence practice is acceptable, THEN structural uncertainty exploitation.
- IF treats disclosure-under-ignorance as validating agreement, THEN fails trust standard.
- IF provides genuine risk info at decision point, THEN positive signal.

# Julia Galef — 5 - Empirical Grounding

> The motivation to see things as they are, not as you wish they were, is the primary determinant of judgment quality. Scout mindset (accuracy-motivated) beats soldier mindset (directionally motivated) regardless of intelligence or knowledge.

*Active period*: 2013-present

## Operating beliefs
- Humans default to soldier mindset; scout mindset requires deliberate cultivation.
- Feeling objective does not make you objective; intelligence does not make you a scout.
- Beliefs are grayscale (probabilistic), not binary.
- Scout status shown by behavior, not self-assessment.
- Self-deception has compounding, delayed costs that are systematically underestimated.
- Present bias distorts beliefs too, soldier mindset pays off immediately, scout benefits accrue over time.
- Emotions are data inputs to rational decision-making, not obstacles (rejection of 'Straw Vulcan' rationality).

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- Finding truth or defending a conclusion?
- 'Can I believe it?' vs 'Must I believe it?', which drives the analysis?
- What would the reasoning look like under different motivations?
- Is confidence calibrated to reality?
- What would change their mind?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Prioritizes reasoning process over domain-specific technical competence.
- Favors explicit uncertainty quantification; may penalize domains where impractical.
- Rewards visible deliberation; may undervalue confident expertise that is genuinely well-calibrated but doesn't show its work.
- Treats individual cognition as primary unit; may miss institutional epistemic failures.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- Individual-level focus. Not designed for institutional/systemic epistemic failures.
- Technical capability gap. Motivation is not always the bottleneck; statistical literacy and domain expertise sometimes are.
- Rapid heuristic domains. Deliberate Bayesian reasoning too slow for emergency/combat decisions.
- Pure values disagreements. Cannot adjudicate terminal values.
- Complex-domain calibration. Binary calibration benchmarks harder to apply to multi-dimensional assessments.
- Contexts with significant power differentials where good faith cannot be assumed.
- Confident expert communication that is genuinely well-calibrated but uses assertive language for audience reasons.
- Scout identity can produce soldier mindset about scout mindset.

## Frameworks

### Scout vs. Soldier Mindset
**Source**: The Scout Mindset (2021); TED Talk (2016)

**Purpose**: Is this deliverable seeking accuracy or defending a position?

Single motivational axis. Scout = mapmaking (accuracy-motivated); soldier = defensive combat (directionally motivated). Six soldier functions: comfort, self-esteem, morale, persuasion, image, belonging. Five behavioral scout signs.

#### Components
- **Motivational Direction** — Driven by 'Is it true?' (scout) or 'Can I believe it? / Must I believe it?' (soldier).
  - Key questions:
    - 'Is it true?' or 'Can/Must I believe this?'
    - Evidence marshaled to find truth or defend conclusion?
    - Conclusions reached before or after evidence?
    - Language of mapping/updating or attacking/defending?
  - Red flags: Explicitly dismisses all counter-evidence and characterizes opponents as irrational/malicious.
- **Behavioral Scout Signs** — Five observable behaviors: (1) telling others when they were right, (2) constructive reaction to criticism, (3) trying to prove yourself wrong, (4) precautions against self-deception, (5) having good critics.
  - Key questions:
    - Prior errors/limitations acknowledged?
    - Criticism preemptively addressed or dismissed?
    - Self-stress-testing visible?
    - Success criteria pre-defined or movable?
    - Credible critics referenced?
- **Soldier Function Detection** — Which protective function is soldier mindset serving: comfort, self-esteem, morale, persuasion, image, or belonging?
  - Key questions:
    - Avoiding uncomfortable conclusions (comfort)?
    - Making author/org look good (self-esteem/image)?
    - Overstating certainty to motivate action (morale)?
    - Optimized to convince rather than find truth (persuasion)?
    - Conforming to group consensus without independent evaluation (belonging)?

#### Decision rules
- IF attack/defend language for own vs opposing views, THEN soldier, penalize Motivation.
- IF appropriate uncertainty and confidence levels distinguished, THEN scout, credit Motivation.
- IF criticism dismissed or critics characterized as unreasonable, THEN soldier, penalize Motivation.
- IF author articulates strongest counter-arguments, THEN scout, credit Motivation.
- IF conclusions preceded evidence examination, THEN soldier, penalize Motivation heavily.

### Five Thought Experiments for Bias Detection
**Source**: The Scout Mindset, Ch. 5

**Purpose**: Is motivated reasoning distorting this analysis?

Five counterfactual tests detecting motivated reasoning by comparing actual reasoning to reasoning under different motivations. Core principle: you cannot detect bias by reviewing your reasoning and finding it logical, you must compare to a counterfactual.

#### Components
- **Double Standard Test** — Same evidentiary standard applied regardless of source? Swap the actor to the 'other side', does evaluation change?
- **Outsider Test** — Would a disinterested outsider reach the same conclusion? Tests sunk cost, emotional attachment, prior commitment.
- **Conformity Test** — Would the conclusion survive if the supporting social group changed its mind? Evidence self-standing or consensus-dependent?
- **Selective Skeptic Test** — If this evidence supported the opposite conclusion, would you still find it credible? Flip conclusion, observe whether methodology assessment changes.
- **Status Quo Bias Test** — If current situation were not the default, would you actively choose it? Tests inertia vs genuine preference.

#### Decision rules
- IF different conclusions for identical evidence depending on source, THEN double standard, penalize Motivation.
- IF different conclusions without personal stake, THEN outsider test failure, penalize Motivation.
- IF conclusion depends on consensus rather than evidence, THEN conformity bias, penalize Evidence Quality.
- IF methodology scrutinized selectively, THEN selective skepticism, penalize Evidence Quality.
- IF recommendation would not be chosen absent inertia, THEN status quo bias, note (may be rational).

### Calibration and Quantifying Uncertainty
**Source**: The Scout Mindset, Ch. 6

**Purpose**: Are confidence levels proportionate to evidence?

Calibration = stated confidence matches actual accuracy rates. Scouts think in shades of gray. Includes: Equivalent Bet Test (compare belief against known probability), Press Secretary vs Board of Directors (claims vs bets).

#### Components
- **Grayscale Belief Expression** — Beliefs on a confidence spectrum, not binary certainties.
  - Key questions:
    - Confidence levels differentiated across claims?
    - Absolute language where hedging warranted?
    - All conclusions treated as equally certain?
  - Red flags: 100% certainty on inherently uncertain empirical claims.
- **Equivalent Bet Test** — Would the author stake real resources at the implied confidence level?
- **Overconfidence and False Precision** — Specific probabilities given without adequate evidential basis? Confidence systematically exceeds evidence?

#### Decision rules
- IF 100% certainty on empirical claims, THEN miscalibrated, penalize Calibration heavily.
- IF all conclusions equally certain regardless of evidence quality, THEN calibration absent, score 1-2.
- IF vague hedging without quantification, THEN weak calibration, score 2-3.
- IF gap between claims and likely bets, THEN press secretary operating, penalize Calibration.

### Bayesian Belief Updating
**Source**: Multiple Galef lectures (2013+); Big Think; Mindscape #143

**Purpose**: Is evidence incorporated correctly or filtered through motivated reasoning?

Bayesian updating as qualitative practice: prior belief + evidence strength = posterior. Five shifts: beliefs are grayscale; ask 'Would a different theory explain it better?'; absence of expected evidence IS evidence; update even on weak evidence; update incrementally.

#### Components
- **Prior Acknowledgment** — What was believed before the evidence? Is the prior grounded in base rates?
  - Key questions:
    - Prior stated or implied?
    - Based on base rates or assumed?
    - Base rate of similar claims considered?
- **Alternative Hypothesis Consideration** — Evidence tested against competing explanations, not just checked for compatibility with favored hypothesis.
  - Key questions:
    - 'Would a different theory explain this better?' or only 'Can my theory explain this?'
    - Alternative hypotheses identified and weighed?
    - Evidence equally likely under multiple hypotheses treated as conclusive for one?
- **Proportional Updating** — Updates proportional to evidence strength, not overweighting single datapoints, not anchoring despite accumulating evidence.
  - Key questions:
    - Proportional updating visible?
    - Counter-evidence and absence of expected evidence treated as evidence?
    - Incremental revision across iterations or fixed conclusions?

#### Decision rules
- IF evidence equally likely under multiple hypotheses treated as conclusive for one, THEN penalize Evidence Quality.
- IF base rate ignored, relying solely on presented evidence, THEN base rate neglect, penalize Evidence Quality.
- IF counter-evidence dismissed without updating, THEN motivated reasoning, penalize Motivation and Evidence Quality.
- IF absence of expected evidence not addressed, THEN asymmetric updating, penalize Evidence Quality.
- IF dramatic change from single datapoint OR no change despite accumulating evidence, THEN penalize Calibration.

### Crux Identification and Double Crux
**Source**: CFAR curriculum; 80,000 Hours podcast; The Update Project

**Purpose**: What are the load-bearing assumptions, and are they well-supported?

A crux = belief that, if changed, changes the conclusion. Double crux = shared load-bearing belief between disagreeing parties. Decomposes arguments into load-bearing assumptions; tests whether stated, falsifiable, and supported.

#### Components
- **Crux Identification** — Key assumptions made explicit and recognized as load-bearing.
  - Key questions:
    - Can you identify beliefs that, if false, invalidate the conclusion?
    - Are cruxes explicitly stated?
    - Load-bearing vs incidental assumptions distinguished?
  - Red flags: Cannot specify what evidence would change conclusion, unfalsifiable.
- **Crux Falsifiability** — Cruxes testable, operationalized, concrete enough to evaluate.
- **Crux Support Quality** — Load-bearing assumptions backed by evidence proportional to their importance.

#### Decision rules
- IF conclusion rests on unstated assumptions that would invalidate it if false, THEN penalize Crux Transparency.
- IF argument dissolved upon operationalizing terms (semantic disagreement), THEN credit Crux Transparency.
- IF cannot specify what evidence would change conclusion, THEN unfalsifiable. FATAL.
- IF competing analyses never identify specific disagreements, THEN double crux not attempted, penalize Crux Transparency.

### The Updating Reframe
**Source**: The Scout Mindset, Ch. 10

**Purpose**: Is belief revision treated as routine maintenance or humiliating failure?

Replaces 'admitting mistake' with 'updating.' An update is routine, makes something better/more current without implying failure. Change mind incrementally; being wrong doesn't mean you screwed up; extract domain-general lessons.

#### Components
- **Update Behavior Presence** — Evidence of beliefs revised in response to new information.
  - Key questions:
    - Revisions from prior positions?
    - Incremental change over time?
    - Language: routine updating or defensive face-saving?

#### Decision rules
- IF all prior claims treated as valid despite new information, THEN updating absent, penalize Motivation.
- IF correction language is defensive, THEN soldier mindset, penalize Motivation.
- IF incremental revisions visible, THEN healthy updating, credit Motivation.

### Epistemic vs. Social Confidence
**Source**: The Scout Mindset, Ch. 9

**Purpose**: Is apparent certainty evidence-based (epistemic) or presentational (social)?

Two habitually conflated confidence types. Epistemic: how sure about what's true. Social: self-assurance in delivery. Influence requires social confidence, not epistemic. Three strategies for uncertainty without incompetence: show uncertainty is justified, give informed estimates, have a plan.

#### Components
- **Confidence Type Discrimination** — Apparent confidence epistemic (evidence-based) or social (presentational)?

#### Decision rules
- IF high certainty + weak evidence, THEN epistemic inflation, penalize Calibration.
- IF appropriate uncertainty + clear analysis, THEN scout combination, credit Calibration and Motivation.
- IF evaluator conflates low epistemic confidence with incompetence, THEN evaluator error, do not penalize uncertainty.

### Holding Identity Lightly
**Source**: The Scout Mindset, Chs. 13-15

**Purpose**: Are conclusions distorted by identity-protective cognition?

Identity-protective cognition distorts reasoning when beliefs become identity badges rather than maps. Signs: 'I believe' as declaration, annoyance at ideology criticism, defiant language, gatekeeping, schadenfreude. Ideological Turing Test: can you represent opposing views convincingly enough to be mistaken for a believer?

#### Components
- **Identity Marker Detection** — Signs beliefs have become identity: righteous tone, us-vs-them, epithets, defiant language, gatekeeping.
  - Key questions:
    - Identity-signaling language?
    - Criticism treated as personal attack?
    - In-group/out-group language suggesting tribal allegiance?
- **Ideological Turing Test** — Can the author represent opposing views convincingly enough that proponents couldn't distinguish them from a genuine believer?
  - Key questions:
    - Strongest opposing arguments engaged or straw-manned?
    - Opposing view in terms proponents would recognize?

#### Decision rules
- IF identity-signaling language present, THEN penalize Identity Management.
- IF cannot pass ideological Turing test, THEN identity too strong, penalize Identity Management.
- IF criticism treated as personal attack, THEN penalize Motivation and Identity Management.
- IF identity held lightly (contingent descriptions, constructive engagement), THEN credit Identity Management.

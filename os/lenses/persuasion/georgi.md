# Stefan Georgi — 1A - Copy Architecture & Persuasion Engineering

> Copy is an engineering problem with architectural prerequisites. Winning copy is produced by executing Research → Mechanism → Brief → Copy in order. Beautiful prose on weak architecture loses to ugly prose on strong architecture.

## Operating beliefs
- Copy channels existing desire by proving pain has a real hidden cause and this product's mechanism targets it.
- The Unique Mechanism is the single most important differentiator in modern DR copy.
- Research quality is the ceiling on copy quality.
- Prospect must never be blamed for past failures, externalize guilt always.
- Product reveal must be delayed until after mechanism is established.
- Proof must be tethered to mechanism claims, not just outcomes.
- The Brief is a technical document with copy-ready chunks, not a loose brainstorm.
- Fascinations are structural load-bearing elements in long-form, not decorative.

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- What format/medium? (VSL, TSL, advertorial, email sequence, landing page)
- Market sophistication level? (How many competing solutions has prospect seen?)
- Prospect awareness level? (Problem-aware? Solution-aware? Product-aware?)
- Is this DR where copy does the full selling job, or supported by brand/referrals/high-intent?
- Is 'hidden cause' credible here, or is the problem already well-understood?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Optimized for DR health/supplement/wealth/info-product niches with corruption and villain framing.
- Strongly favors long-form over short-form, entire system assumes sequential reading.
- Prioritizes emotional agitation and curiosity; underweights clarity-first approaches.
- Treats 'hidden cause' as near-universal, but some markets have well-understood problems.
- Mechanism-centric scoring can unfairly penalize copy that converts via social proof or brand trust.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- Short-form (<300 words): Cannot execute full architecture. Only applies to single hook + mechanism hint + proof + CTA.
- Enterprise B2B: Corruption narratives read as manipulative in committee-driven buying. Framework must reframe as 'root cause diagnosis.'
- SaaS clarity pages: These convert through immediate product visibility + UX. Penalizing early product display is incorrect for high-intent traffic.
- Well-understood problems: No credible 'hidden cause' exists. Mechanism framework must shift to implementation differentiation.
- Guilt externalization inappropriate: Some categories require accountability framing (addiction recovery, compliance).
- Brand-driven markets: Mechanism-heavy language clashes with premium brand voice.
- Formulaic VSL voice: Strict RMBC can sound like ClickBank regardless of context. May need reduced fascination density and increased proof.
- Does not cover offer economics, UX friction, conversion design, claims compliance, or JTBD messaging.

## Frameworks

### RMBC Method
**Source**: rmbcmethod.com; stefanpaulgeorgi.com; Scribd notes

**Purpose**: Master framework: was copy built correctly from upstream inputs?

Four-phase sequential method: Research → Mechanism → Brief → Copy. Each phase produces specific outputs that feed the next.

#### Components
- **Research Phase** — Produces market truths: prospect language, real pains, existing solutions, horror stories, beliefs, corruption narratives.
  - Key questions:
    - Specific avatar identity markers vs generic 'if you struggle with X'?
    - Real alternatives named with actual experiences described?
    - Outside forces/corruption framing native to market, not invented?
    - Horror stories from wrong solutions, specific and plausible?
    - Language sounds market-lifted (forums, reviews) vs marketer language?
    - 60%+ of 22 Research Questions reflected as specific details?
- **Mechanism Phase** — Produces two-part Unique Mechanism: Problem (hidden real cause) + Solution (method targeting that cause), logically linked.
  - Key questions:
    - See Unique Mechanism Architecture framework.
- **Brief Phase** — Structured blueprint merging research + mechanism into copy-ready chunks. Components: Audience definition, Pain points + biggest fears, Big promise (pains reversed), Existing solutions + why they failed, Root cause + surprising info + how solution works, Paradoxical question, Metaphor, Bold claim, Product features/benefits with fascinations, Background + discovery story, Headline ideas.
  - Key questions:
    - One coherent big promise and mechanism throughout, or competing spines?
    - Sections feel preplanned vs discovered mid-draft?
    - Contradictions, sudden new angles, or abandoned loops?
- **Copy Phase** — Final asset following fixed macro sequence: Lead → Background Story → Unique Mechanism of Problem → Unique Mechanism of Solution → Product Build-Up and Reveal → Close → FAQs.
  - Key questions:
    - All 7 sections present (or compressed for shorter format)?
    - Order intact?
    - Transitions logical, paying off open loops?
    - Product reveal delayed until after mechanisms?
    - Close earns the ask through accumulated belief?
  - Red flags: Product revealed before mechanisms → premature selling

#### Decision rules
- IF research thin → mechanism will be generic, copy sounds like 'marketer copy.' Flag upstream.
- IF mechanism missing/not unique → fundamental failure.
- IF brief undisciplined → sections wander, contradictions appear.
- IF sequence rearranged (product before mechanisms) → fundamental failure.
- IF prose beautiful but mechanism absent → 'nice but not engineered,' will lose in competitive market.

### The 22 Research Questions
**Source**: RMBC course; Scribd doc 646012392; mastermind notes

**Purpose**: 22-question research protocol. Each unanswered question = weak corresponding copy element.

Four categories of research questions that map directly to copy elements.

#### Components
- **Demographic Beliefs (RQ1-7)** — Avatar identity, attitudes, hopes, victories, outside forces, prejudices, core beliefs.
  - Key questions:
    - RQ1: Who is avatar concretely? → Informs language, scenes, credibility choices.
    - RQ2: What attitudes (religious, political, social)? → Informs acceptable villains/authorities, taboos.
    - RQ3: Hopes and dreams? → Informs big promise, future pacing.
    - RQ4: Victories and failures? → Informs guilt externalization, proof selection.
    - RQ5: Outside forces blocking them? → Informs villains, corruption, mechanism framing.
    - RQ6: Prejudices and strong dislikes? → Informs objection handling, tone.
    - RQ7: Core beliefs about life? → Informs emotional arc, moral framing.
- **Existing Solutions (RQ8-14)** — Alternatives used, experiences with them, likes/dislikes, horror stories, beliefs about effectiveness.
  - Key questions:
    - RQ8: What alternatives are they using? → Informs differentiation, comparisons.
    - RQ9: Experience with those alternatives? → Informs agitation, skepticism, proof needs.
    - RQ10: What do they LIKE about alternatives? → Informs positioning to preserve valued elements.
    - RQ11: What do they DISLIKE? → Informs mechanism features.
    - RQ12: Horror stories about alternatives? → Informs fear proof, safety positioning.
    - RQ13: Do they believe existing solutions work? → Informs awareness level, education needed.
    - RQ14: If not, why not? → Informs knowledge gap setup.
- **Curiosity Mining (RQ15-19)** — Unique historical solutions, conspiratorial explanations, pre-1960 attempts.
  - Key questions:
    - RQ15: Has someone solved it uniquely before? → Informs mechanism inspiration, lead angles.
    - RQ16: Result of that unique attempt? → Informs mechanism credibility.
    - RQ17: Conspiratorial explanation for old solutions failing? → Informs villain narrative, indignation.
    - RQ18: Pre-1960 unique attempts? → Informs authority via history.
    - RQ19: What happened to those old attempts? → Informs 'lost solution' story.
- **Corruption Framing (RQ20-22)** — Whether the problem is worsening due to outside forces with legible motives.
  - Key questions:
    - RQ20: Did the problem used to not exist/wasn't as bad? → Informs urgency, externalization.
    - RQ21: Outside forces recently made it worse? → Informs villains, mechanism plausibility.
    - RQ22: What are those forces and their motives? → Informs credibility, narrative logic.

#### Decision rules
- IF <60% reflected as specific details → research is shallow.
- IF no alternatives or why they failed → can't credibly claim 'this is different.'
- IF corruption used but disconnected from mechanism → gratuitous rage, no purpose.

### Unique Mechanism Architecture
**Source**: stefanpaulgeorgi.com (lead checklist, RMBC method); Scribd notes

**Purpose**: The core differentiator: unique causal explanation for pain + unique causal solution, logically connected.

Two-part mechanism (problem + solution) connected by a causal chain, presented in three levels (tease, explain, substantiate).

#### Components
- **Problem Mechanism** — The hidden 'real cause': the missing 1% explaining why they've struggled.
  - Key questions:
    - Is it specific (named process/force/failure mode)?
    - Is it novel (not category default)?
    - Is it plausible (survives basic skepticism)?
    - Is it absolution-oriented (not reader's fault)?
    - Is it proof-supported at point of claim?
    - Does it use clear 'missing piece' framing?
  - Red flags: Absent entirely,Blames prospect for past failure
- **Solution Mechanism** — Concrete method directly targeting the problem mechanism.
  - Key questions:
    - Does it directly target the problem mechanism (not generic 'supports health')?
    - Is implementation concrete (ingredient, process, system, protocol)?
    - Does proof support WHY it works, not just that it works?
    - Is it defensible, competitor can't swap in their product?
    - Is it teachable, prospect could repeat the explanation?
- **Causal Chain** — Problem Mechanism → Old Solutions Failed → Solution Mechanism → Outcome. If chain breaks at any arrow, mechanism is cosmetic.
  - Key questions:
    - Could a competitor swap in their product without changing the causal story? If yes → not unique.
- **Presentation Levels** — Agent inference from Georgi's pattern, not his confirmed taxonomy. Three levels: Tease (hint hidden cause in lead), Explain (lay out causal story in mechanism sections), Substantiate (prove it with evidence at point of claim).
  - Key questions:
    - Is the mechanism teased in the lead?
    - Is the causal story fully explained in mechanism sections?
    - Is evidence provided at point of claim?

#### Decision rules
- IF no 'real cause' story → absent, fundamental failure.
- IF cause = 'you're not disciplined' → anti-Georgi, fails absolution.
- IF solution generic → weak, no defensible differentiation.
- IF product revealed before mechanism framed → premature selling.
- IF mechanism present but no proof at claim point → 'asserted, not established,' weakness.

### 7-Item Headline Checklist
**Source**: Scribd doc 819843963 (Writing Killer Headlines module)

**Purpose**: Headline must capture attention and frame promise without collapsing curiosity.

Seven elements that a strong headline should incorporate.

#### Components
- **Curiosity** — Creates information gap reader wants closed.
- **Pain callout** — Prospect thinks 'that's me.'
- **Solution promise** — Implies path to resolution without revealing mechanism.
- **Specificity** — Numbers, timeframes, conditions that make it non-interchangeable.
- **Credibility** — Authority signals or skepticism acknowledgment.
- **Timeframe** — When can they expect the outcome.
- **Simplicity** — Understood on first read, no jargon.

#### Decision rules
- IF no pain + no promise → branding, not DR.
- IF not specific → interchangeable, any competitor could use it.
- IF complex → doesn't get read.

### Lead Structure and Diagnostics
**Source**: Scribd notes; Georgi blog: Throat Clearing, Forgetting to Fascinate, Selling Too Soon

**Purpose**: Lead's job is continuation and connection, NOT conversion.

Five components of an effective lead, plus six lead type archetypes.

#### Components
- **Hook immediacy** — First 1-3 sentences demand attention. No throat clearing.
  - Red flags: Wasted lines before hook
- **Pain + promise** — Pain named specifically, promise clear without revealing mechanism.
- **Fascination density** — 3+ curiosity bullets framed as riddles, mechanism-tied, specific, paid off later.
  - Red flags: Zero fascinations → engagement sags in long-form
- **Mechanism tease** — Hints hidden cause or 'missing piece' without explaining.
- **No premature selling** — Product NOT named, no purchase CTAs, reader thinking about discovery not product.
  - Red flags: Product sold in lead

#### Decision rules
- IF hook delayed → throat clearing, reader drops off.
- IF no fascinations → engagement sags in long-form.
- IF product appears in lead → premature selling, fundamental failure.

### Proof Sequencing
**Source**: RMBC outline (Scribd), proof placement within structural sections

**Purpose**: Proof must appear where skepticism spikes (mechanism claims), not float randomly or cluster at close.

Maps where proof should appear across the five structural sections of a sales asset.

#### Components
- **Lead proof** — Light credibility builders, broad testimonials, no product reveal.
- **Problem mechanism proof** — Proof elements establishing the cause (studies, authority, logic).
- **Solution mechanism proof** — Proof establishing solution plausibility. WHY it works.
- **Product section proof** — Product-specific proof, social spread ('others asking for it').
- **Close proof** — Testimonials, value comparison, guarantee, urgency.

#### Decision rules
- IF proof is outcomes-only ('I lost 20 lbs') with no mechanism tie → weak.
- IF proof missing at mechanism claims → 'asserted, not established.'
- IF proof only in close → placed too late, skepticism already killed engagement.

### Emotional Architecture
**Source**: Agent inference from Georgi's structural ordering + explicit statements. NOT a formal named Georgi framework.

**Purpose**: Emotional flow must match structural section jobs.

Maps expected emotional tone to each structural section.

#### Components
- **Lead** — Emotional target: curiosity + empathy.
- **Background** — Emotional target: identification + trust.
- **Problem mechanism** — Emotional target: indignation + relief ('not your fault').
- **Solution mechanism** — Emotional target: hope + control.
- **Product buildup** — Emotional target: anticipation + 'finally'.
- **Close** — Emotional target: urgency + self-efficacy.
- **FAQs** — Emotional target: reassurance.

#### Decision rules
- IF agitation not tied to mechanism → manipulation noise.
- IF hope arrives before cause established → belief is weak.
- IF urgency before value proven → feels like pressure.

### Subject Line Matrix (Email)
**Source**: 2019 Mastermind notes

**Purpose**: Email subject lines should rotate across categories matching prospect journey stage.

11 subject line categories to rotate through: promise, problem, new cause discovered, new mechanism, taboo, dominant emotion, story, conflict/conspiracy, challenge/shock, urgency/scarcity, targeted.

#### Components
- **Category rotation** — 4+ categories in a sequence. Curiosity early, proof middle, urgency late. All same category → monotone, engagement decays.

#### Decision rules
- IF fewer than 4 categories in a sequence → monotone, engagement decays.
- IF all same category → flat engagement curve.

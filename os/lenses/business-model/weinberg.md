# Gabriel Weinberg — 12 - Business Model Viability

> Poor distribution, not product, is the #1 cause of startup failure. One working distribution channel = great business. The math works when acquisition happens through at least one scalable channel at sustainable cost relative to revenue.

*Active period*: 2014 (Traction book; DuckDuckGo founder)

## Operating beliefs
- Failed startups have products. They lack customers. Distribution failure > product failure.
- At any stage one channel dominates. Most get zero to work. Goal: find that one.
- Traction and product in parallel, 50/50. 'Build first, market later' = Product Trap.
- Can't know which channel works by thinking. Must test. Founders biased toward familiar channels.
- Growth is non-linear: slow start, spike at channel unlock, flatten at saturation, spike again.
- All channels saturate (Law of Shitty Click-Throughs). Never assume permanence.
- Traction = quantitative customer demand evidence. Anecdotes and 'good feedback' are not traction.
- The right traction goal dictates direction. Everything else serves traction.

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- Traction goal? Quantifiable target that changes things significantly.
- Phase? I (seeking PMF), II (scaling proven fit), III (dominating)?
- Revenue model? Usage -> revenue path?
- CAC and LTV? CAC < LTV with margin?
- Addressable market? Volume sufficient for breakeven?
- Channels tested? Results? Dismissed untested?
- Product vs traction time split?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Acquisition-heavy: Weights acquisition over retention, engagement, monetization optimization.
- Channel-sequential: One at a time; may underweight multichannel/compound growth.
- Startup-centric: Less calibrated for established businesses.
- Consumer/SaaS skew: Enterprise, marketplace, hardware get less depth.
- 2014 landscape: Predates TikTok, creator marketing, PLG, AI distribution.
- Anti-intuition: Testing over domain expertise. May undervalue operator judgment.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- Retention/engagement: Emphasizes acquisition. Cohort analysis, churn get no treatment. Can score well while losing customers.
- Pricing/monetization: Mentions 'users won't pay' but no WTP testing process.
- Unit economics depth: CAC vs LTV identified but no LTV calculation, payback, or margin frameworks.
- Marketplace dynamics: Sequential single-channel may not suit chicken-and-egg requiring simultaneous supply/demand.
- Pre-product stage: Bullseye assumes product exists.
- Regulated markets: 'Test all 19' may be impractical where legally constrained.
- Multichannel synergy: 'One dominates' may penalize legitimate orchestrated multichannel.
- Product-led growth where product IS distribution may appear to lack channel strategy.
- No venture-scale vs lifestyle-business distinction.

## Frameworks

### Business Viability Four-Check
**Source**: Traction Ch.1

**Purpose**: Fundamental viability before evaluating traction strategy.

Four conditions must ALL hold. Each a distinct math problem. Any single failure is potentially fatal.

#### Components
- **Revenue Model Viability** — Path from usage to revenue?
  - Key questions:
    - Clearly articulated?
    - WTP validated not assumed?
    - Revenue at current usage or requires massive scale?
  - Red flags: No model AND no plan to test. Revenue = 0, no path.
- **Market Size Sufficiency** — Enough customers for profitability? Volume > breakeven threshold.
  - Key questions:
    - TAM bottom-up (not '1% of $X billion')?
    - Volume exceeds breakeven?
    - Growing, stable, or shrinking?
  - Red flags: Demonstrably too small, no expansion path.
- **Acquisition Cost Sustainability** — CAC < LTV. Affordable customer reach.
  - Key questions:
    - CAC measured or estimated?
    - LTV from cohort data?
    - CAC < LTV with margin?
    - Affordable channel evident?
  - Red flags: CAC > LTV all tested channels, no viable alternatives.
- **Competitive Distribution Differentiation** — Distribution differentiation? Saturation dilutes share below viability.
  - Key questions:
    - Direct competitors?
    - Underused/inaccessible channels?
    - Distribution advantage?
    - Dilution threat?
  - Red flags: Identical product, saturated market, no angle, incumbents own all channels.

#### Decision rules
- IF revenue = 0 with no plan, THEN FATAL.
- IF market < breakeven, no expansion, THEN FATAL.
- IF CAC > LTV all tested, THEN FATAL unless untested channels remain.
- IF saturated + identical + no angle, THEN FATAL.
- IF 1 fails but addressable, THEN flag risk, prescribe test.
- IF all 4 pass, THEN proceed to channel strategy.

### Bullseye Framework
**Source**: Traction Ch.2

**Purpose**: Optimal channel through systematic testing, not assumption.

Five-step repeatable process. Three rings: Outer (all 19 brainstormed), Middle (top 3 under test), Inner (one focused). One channel dominates at any stage.

#### Components
- **Brainstorm Completeness** — Idea for each of 19 channels, none dismissed.
  - Key questions:
    - How many of 19?
    - Unfamiliar dismissed untested (channel bias)?
    - Spreadsheet: ideas, probability, CAC, volume, timeframe?
    - Comparable histories researched?
- **Testing Rigor (Middle Ring)** — Top 3: cheap fast tests answering cost, volume, quality.
  - Key questions:
    - 3 parallel?
    - <$1K, <1 month each?
    - Three questions answered?
    - Goal: IF channel works, not traction yet?
    - Tracking before testing?
- **Focus Discipline** — All resources on one winner, optimizing.
  - Key questions:
    - One clear core channel?
    - Regular A/B (1/week = 2-3x)?
    - Scattered without priority?
    - Rerun plan at saturation?
- **Channel-Phase Alignment** — Strategy matches growth phase.
  - Key questions:
    - Phase I: Non-scalable?
    - Phase II: Scalable with PMF?
    - Phase III: High-volume?
    - Leaky bucket (Phase I product + Phase III spend)?

#### Decision rules
- IF no channels tested, THEN assumptions only. Penalize heavily.
- IF only familiar channels, THEN channel bias. Penalize.
- IF tests miss cost/volume/quality, THEN unfocused. Partial credit.
- IF scaling leaky bucket, THEN phase mismatch. Penalize.
- IF winner exists but effort scattered, THEN focus failure. Penalize.
- IF never rerun after saturation, THEN incomplete. Flag.
- IF all middle ring failed, THEN evaluate brainstorm and test quality first.

### 50% Rule
**Source**: Traction Ch.3

**Purpose**: Adequate traction allocation or Product Trap?

50% product, 50% traction, in parallel. Product Trap: 'build it, they will come.' Traction generates product-informing data.

#### Components
- **Resource Allocation Balance** — ~50/50 product-traction split.
  - Key questions:
    - % product vs traction?
    - Traction now or 'after launch'?
    - Traction insights feeding product?

#### Decision rules
- IF 100/0, THEN Product Trap. Penalize.
- IF traction deferred post-launch, THEN Product Trap variant.
- IF traction feeding product, THEN bonus, virtuous cycle.
- IF Dropbox pattern (pre-launch testing revealing CAC problems), THEN strong adherence.

### Three Phases of Traction
**Source**: Traction Ch.3

**Purpose**: Phase identification and phase-appropriate strategy.

I: Making something people want (non-scalable, bright spots). II: Marketing it (scalable, PMF achieved). III: Scaling (high-volume, dominate). Leaky Bucket: don't scale Phase I, small amounts to find and plug holes.

#### Components
- **Phase Identification** — Current phase?
  - Key questions:
    - I: Early, no PMF, high churn, seeking bright spots.
    - II: PMF achieved, churn dropping, need scalable distribution.
    - III: Established, profitable unit economics, need volume.
- **Bright Spots (Phase I)** — Pockets of engaged users? Expand from base. None = consider pivot.
  - Key questions:
    - Highly engaged subgroup?
    - Understand why?
    - Replicating conditions?
- **Leaky Bucket** — Scaling before product retains?
  - Key questions:
    - Churn/retention?
    - Acquired users staying?
    - Spending on acquisition without retention?

#### Decision rules
- IF Phase I + expensive scalable channels, THEN leaky bucket. Penalize.
- IF Phase I + no bright spots, THEN pivot signal. Flag critical.
- IF Phase II + only non-scalable, THEN transition failure. Penalize.
- IF Phase III + Phase I channels, THEN evolution failure. Penalize.
- IF flatlined, THEN check saturation and Bullseye rerun.

### Critical Path Concept
**Source**: Traction Ch.5

**Purpose**: Fewest steps to traction goal or scattered?

Quantifiable goal -> backward milestones -> execute step one -> re-assess. On-path or off-path. Off-path = don't do it.

#### Components
- **Traction Goal** — Quantifiable target that changes things significantly.
  - Key questions:
    - Quantified (1K paying, 100 DAU, 10% share)?
    - Work-backward specific?
    - Ambitious but achievable?
- **Path Discipline** — On-path activities; off-path eliminated.
  - Key questions:
    - Activities trace to milestones?
    - Off-path resource consumption?
    - Re-assessing after each step?

#### Decision rules
- IF no goal, THEN lacks direction. Penalize.
- IF goal but disconnected activities, THEN execution gap. Penalize.
- IF re-assesses after milestones, THEN strong discipline.
- IF adaptations visible ('original plan often wrong'), THEN bonus.

### Viral Marketing Math
**Source**: Traction Ch.6

**Purpose**: If viral claimed, does math support it?

K = invites/user x conversion%. K>1 exponential, K>0.5 strong assist, K<0.5 insufficient. Cycle Time equally critical, shorter = dramatically faster. Six loop types: WOM, inherent, collaborative, incentivized, embedded/communicative, social leverage.

#### Components
- **Viral Coefficient (K)** — K = invites x CTR x signup%.
  - Key questions:
    - K from real data or assumed?
    - Value?
    - Weakest step identified?
    - Loop seeded?
- **Cycle Time** — Time per loop completion. Shorter = dramatically faster.
  - Key questions:
    - Measured?
    - Loop mapped, steps removed?
    - Friction eliminated?
- **Loop Type** — Which of six types? Right for product?
  - Key questions:
    - Type identified?
    - Matches natural usage?
    - Viral pockets targeted?

#### Decision rules
- IF viral claimed, K unmeasured, THEN unvalidated. Penalize.
- IF K<0.5 as primary strategy, THEN insufficient. Suggest alternatives.
- IF K>0.5 but cycle >7 days, THEN too slow. Optimize cycle.
- IF 'we'll go viral' without mechanics/math, THEN fantasy. Penalize heavily.
- IF incentivized loop with measured K + short cycle (Dropbox), THEN strong.

### Traction Testing Methodology
**Source**: Traction Ch.4

**Purpose**: Testing with rigor or guessing?

Middle Ring: IF channel works, <$1K, <1 month. Inner Ring: optimize chosen, ongoing. Metrics: CAC/CPA, LTV, CPC, conversion, volume, quality. All channels saturate, constant experimentation required.

#### Components
- **Test Design Quality** — Validate/invalidate specific assumptions with constraints.
  - Key questions:
    - Clear hypothesis?
    - Middle ring <$1K, <1 month?
    - Tracking before tests?
    - Speed to data, not perfection?
- **Core Metrics** — Essential metrics per channel.
  - Key questions:
    - CAC/CPA per channel?
    - LTV?
    - Funnel conversion?
    - Customer quality?
    - Dashboard with time series?
- **Optimization Cadence** — Systematic optimization of winner.
  - Key questions:
    - A/B weekly (2-3x efficiency)?
    - Focused sprints per metric?
    - Saturation awareness?

#### Decision rules
- IF no pre-test tracking, THEN unreliable. Penalize.
- IF middle ring >$1K or >1 month, THEN overbuilt. Flag.
- IF no A/B on winner, THEN 2-3x efficiency lost. Penalize.
- IF unchanged 6+ months, THEN saturation risk. Flag.
- IF tests miss cost/volume/quality, THEN unfocused. Partial credit.

### Moving the Needle
**Source**: Traction Ch.3

**Purpose**: Each activity creating significant measurable impact at current scale?

Phase-dependent thresholds: Phase I, single meetup moves needle. Phase II-III, need millions; at 1-5% conversion, 100K customers = 2-10M reach.

#### Components
- **Needle-Moving Assessment** — Activity plausibly significant at current stage?
  - Key questions:
    - What volume = needle at current stage?
    - Activity delivers that volume?
    - Measurable, not 'brand building' without metrics?

#### Decision rules
- IF can't move needle at stage, THEN don't pursue. Penalize.
- IF Phase III pursuing hundreds-reach, THEN mismatched. Penalize.
- IF Phase I dismissing non-scalable, THEN premature. Non-scalable is correct.

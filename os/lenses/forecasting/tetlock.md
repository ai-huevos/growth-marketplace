# Philip Tetlock — 11 - Strategic Reasoning

> Upstream thinking quality is measurable through calibration, cognitive style, and epistemic practice. How you think matters more than what you think, foxes who aggregate multiple models, update frequently, and anchor in base rates consistently outperform hedgehogs organized around one Big Idea.

## Operating beliefs
- Cognitive style is the strongest accuracy predictor, stronger than experience, ideology, or domain expertise.
- Outside view first. Anchor in base rates from reference classes before case-specific adjustment.
- Beliefs are hypotheses, not treasures. Perpetual beta is ~3x as powerful a predictor of accuracy as intelligence.
- Vague verbiage ('likely,' 'serious risk') destroys accountability. Translate hunches into numeric probabilities.
- Each commandment requires balancing opposing errors. Overcorrection is as dangerous as the original bias.
- Nothing is 100% unique. There is always a reference class.
- Process accountability to an audience with unknown views produces the most self-critical thinking.
- You can't become a superforecaster by reading manuals. Learning requires doing, with good feedback.

## Key questions (use as self-check before finalizing output)
- Claims about future, causation, or prescriptive action? (If pure description, evaluate on integrative complexity + accountability.)
- Time horizon? (Under 2yr: full applicability. 2-5yr: apply with caution. Over 5yr: diminished applicability.)
- Goldilocks zone? Neither clocklike (simple rules suffice) nor cloud-like (fundamentally unpredictable)?
- Audience for accountability? Known views bias toward ingratiation; unknown views promote self-critical thinking.
- Sacred values in play that might constrain the boundaries of the thinkable?

## Biases (disclose in output footer)
- Favors probabilistic, quantified reasoning. May undervalue qualitative insight or expert intuition resisting quantification.
- Privileges fox-like style. May unfairly penalize deep specialists whose narrow expertise is genuinely superior in well-modeled domains.
- Calibration-centric: may overweight confidence-evidence match at expense of whether right questions were asked.
- Validated primarily on geopolitical forecasting (1-12 month horizons). Application to business strategy or longer horizons is extrapolation.
- Treats sacred-value protection as reasoning failure. Creates tension where moral commitments are legitimately non-negotiable.

## Limitations (suppress lens application in these contexts)
- BLIND: Horizons beyond 2 years. EPJ accuracy declined toward chance at 3-5yr, indistinguishable from chance at 10+yr.
- BLIND: Black swans. Forecasting tournaments poorly suited to rare, high-impact events lacking adequate reference classes.
- BLIND: Question-type dependency. IARPA questions were rigorously resolvable binary/multinomial. Real strategic reasoning involves ambiguous, multi-dimensional problems.
- BLIND: Perpetual beta may be stable personality trait rather than teachable skill. May penalize thinkers with fixed cognitive style but otherwise sound reasoning.
- BLIND: Domain-specificity. Fox/hedgehog validated on geopolitics. Whether it applies equally to technical domains where deep specialization has more value is unestablished.
- UNFAIR PENALTY: Even best superforecasters only modestly beat simple algorithms or well-aggregated crowds. May overvalue individual cognitive virtues vs. structural solutions.
- UNFAIR PENALTY: Superforecasting requires deliberation time. Under extreme time pressure, different strategies may be optimal.

## Frameworks

### Superforecasting: Ten Commandments
**Source**: Superforecasting (2015), pp. 277-289; Good Judgment Project

**Purpose**: Does upstream reasoning exhibit cognitive practices experimentally demonstrated to boost forecasting accuracy?

Eleven commandments operationalizing superforecaster cognitive habits. Each requires balancing opposing errors, overcorrection is as dangerous as the original bias.

#### Components
- **Triage (C1)** — Focus on Goldilocks zone between clocklike (rule-solvable) and cloud-like (unpredictable). Two errors: failing to try on tractable questions, or wasting effort on intractable ones.
  - Key questions:
    - Distinguished tractable vs. intractable sub-questions?
    - Resources concentrated where forecasting effort pays off?
    - Avoided asserting confidence on radically unpredictable components?
- **Fermi-izing (C2)** — Break intractable problems into tractable sub-problems. Specify target, decompose, estimate components, combine. Beware substituting easy question for hard one.
  - Key questions:
    - Central claim decomposed into sub-questions with separable estimates?
    - Assumptions for each component explicit?
    - Avoided question substitution?
    - Knowable/unknowable parts separated?
- **Inside/Outside View Balance (C3)** — Anchor with base rates from reference classes (outside view) first, then adjust with case-specific info (inside view). 'How often do things of this sort happen in situations of this sort?' Nothing is 100% unique.
  - Key questions:
    - Reference class identified? Base rate cited?
    - Case-specific reasoning adjusts from base rate rather than replaces it?
    - Avoided unjustified 'this time is different'?
    - Reference class reasonable, or 'reference class tennis' happening?
- **Belief Updating (C4)** — Approach truth via proportional updates. Usually many small; occasionally big when warranted. Resist wishful thinking.
  - Key questions:
    - Evidence of iterative revision or one-shot pronouncement?
    - Update triggers identified?
    - Provisional or final framing?
    - Distinguishes signal from noise?
- **Clashing Causal Forces (C5)** — Dragonfly-eye: consider problem from multiple angles. List reasons for and against. Devil's Advocate. 'On the one hand... on the other... on the third hand.'
  - Key questions:
    - How many competing causal models?
    - Pro AND con arguments explicitly listed?
    - Devil's Advocate reasoning present?
    - Other thoughtful observers' views surveyed?
- **Granularity of Uncertainty (C6)** — Translate vague hunches into numeric probabilities. Distinguish as many degrees of doubt as the problem permits. Advantage comes from separating 60/40 from 40/60.
  - Key questions:
    - Claims expressed with numeric probabilities?
    - Vague verbiage avoided or translated?
    - Granularity appropriate, no false precision on cloud-like, not vague on tractable?
    - Could someone be held accountable against these statements?
- **Calibration-Resolution Balance (C7)** — Good scores require both calibration (confidence matches reality) and resolution (discriminating outcomes). Manage boldness/caution trade-off. Avoid both misses and false alarms.
  - Key questions:
    - Stated confidence matches evidence strength?
    - Meaningful distinctions between more/less likely outcomes, or fence-sitting at 50%?
    - Boldness/caution appropriate for stakes?
- **Postmortem Thinking (C8)** — Unflinching postmortems on failures and successes. Beware hindsight bias. Not all successes imply correct reasoning, may have lucked out via offsetting errors.
  - Key questions:
    - Examines own potential failure modes?
    - Past analogous predictions examined?
    - Distinguishes right-for-right-reasons vs. right-by-luck?
    - Hindsight bias controlled?
- **Collaborative Reasoning (C9)** — Perspective taking, precision questioning, constructive confrontation. Reproduce opposing arguments to the other's satisfaction.
  - Key questions:
    - Opposing arguments steelmanned?
    - Precision questioning of ambiguous terms?
    - Diverse perspectives incorporated?

#### Decision rules
- IF no reference class/base rate for predictive claim, THEN Inside-View Dominance (C3 failure). Impact: -3 to -5.
- IF only one causal model with no alternatives, THEN Hedgehog Reasoning (C5 failure). Impact: -3 to -5.
- IF all claims use vague verbiage, THEN Insufficient Granularity (C6 failure). Impact: -3 to -5.
- IF conclusions predetermined with no revision mechanism, THEN Perpetual Beta Failure (C4). Impact: -3 to -5.
- IF bold predictions without proportional evidence, THEN Overconfidence (C7 failure). Potential fatal.
- IF analysis avoids certain trade-offs or refuses base rates, THEN Sacred Value Constraint (SVPM). Impact: -3 to -5.

### Fox vs. Hedgehog Cognitive Style
**Source**: Expert Political Judgment (2005); Isaiah Berlin 1953; 284 experts, ~28,000 predictions over ~20 years

**Purpose**: Reasoning organized around one Big Idea (hedgehog) or aggregated from multiple micro-theories with tentative conclusions (fox)?

Fox/hedgehog was the strongest EPJ accuracy predictor, stronger than experience, ideology, or domain expertise. About cognitive style, not intelligence or knowledge.

#### Components
- **Orientation** — Hedgehogs: One Big Idea. Foxes: multiple micro-theories aggregated.
  - Key questions:
    - Single organizing thesis driving all conclusions? (Hedgehog.)
    - Multiple models from different disciplines? (Fox.)
    - Analysis adapts when problem changes, or always returns to same framework?
- **Confidence Calibration** — Hedgehogs: bold predictions (near 0%/100%). Foxes: cautious, diffident, appropriate spread.
  - Key questions:
    - Predictions clustered at extremes or appropriately distributed?
    - Assertive certainty ('will,' 'clearly') or calibrated uncertainty ('likely,' with probability)?
    - Qualifiers genuine or performative?
- **Error Response** — Hedgehogs explain away failures, update slowly. Foxes admit mistakes, update rapidly.
  - Key questions:
    - Prior errors acknowledged?
    - Disconfirming evidence incorporated (fox) or explained away (hedgehog)?
    - Defensive mechanisms: 'almost right,' 'timing off,' 'exogenous shock'?
- **Integrative Complexity** — Differentiation (recognizing multiple perspectives) + integration (connecting them). Scale 1-7. Higher IC = fox-like, better accuracy.
  - Key questions:
    - How many distinct perspectives recognized?
    - Connections drawn between them or listed without synthesis?
    - Genuine integration or mere juxtaposition?

#### Decision rules
- IF single thesis drives all conclusions + contrary evidence dismissed, THEN Hedgehog Reasoning. Expect poor calibration.
- IF assertively certain with bold predictions and no qualifiers, THEN flag overconfidence consistent with hedgehog style.
- IF IC 5+, multiple frameworks, tentative conclusions, THEN Fox-like. Expect better calibration.
- IF media-friendly simplicity (bold narrative, hero/villain) substitutes for analysis, THEN flag hedgehog-driven media bias.

### Reference Class Forecasting
**Source**: Superforecasting; Kahneman-Tversky-Tetlock; CHAMPS-KNOW (C = Comparison Classes, only principle significantly positively correlated with GJP success)

**Purpose**: Has reasoning anchored in base rates from similar past situations before adjusting with case-specific information?

Three steps: (1) Identify reference class of similar past situations. (2) Obtain base rate. (3) Adjust from baseline with case-specific info. Comparison classes were the ONLY GJP principle significantly positively correlated with success.

#### Components
- **Reference Class Identification** — A set of similar past situations providing a statistical anchor.
  - Key questions:
    - Reference class explicitly stated?
    - Appropriate, similar enough to be informative, not so broad as to be meaningless?
    - 'Reference class tennis' avoided?
- **Base Rate Anchoring** — Statistical frequency of outcome in the reference class.
  - Key questions:
    - Base rate cited with source?
    - From reliable, relevant dataset?
    - Reasoning anchored TO it (adjusting from) rather than mentioning then ignoring?
- **Inside-View Adjustment** — Case-specific info justifying shift from base rate, with direction and magnitude stated.
  - Key questions:
    - Case-specific factors justify departing from base rate?
    - Direction stated?
    - Magnitude proportional or does it overwhelm the base rate?

#### Decision rules
- IF predictive claim has no reference class and no base rate, THEN Inside-View Dominance. Most common and costly reasoning failure.
- IF base rate cited then overridden by narrative ('our case is unique because...'), THEN Base Rate Neglect.
- IF multiple reference classes debated without resolution, THEN flag Reference Class Tennis. Should acknowledge chosen class and why.

### Sacred Value Protection Model (SVPM)
**Source**: Tetlock (2000) JPSP; (2003) Trends in Cognitive Sciences; Social Functionalist Framework (2002)

**Purpose**: Are moral-political commitments distorting analysis by placing trade-offs, base rates, or counterfactuals off-limits?

Three proscribed cognition categories communities forbid, plus three social-functionalist modes (politician, theologian, prosecutor) that degrade reasoning when replacing scientist mode.

#### Components
- **Taboo Trade-offs** — Monetizing/trading sacred vs. secular values triggers outrage. Thinker refuses to consider trade-off rather than analyzing it.
  - Key questions:
    - Trade-offs refused?
    - Resource allocation treated as purely moral rather than also empirical?
    - Acknowledges that refusing explicit trade-offs means making them implicitly?
- **Forbidden Base Rates** — Using base rates implicating protected groups or challenging sacred narratives triggers anger. Thinker refuses available statistical evidence.
  - Key questions:
    - Relevant base rates avoided?
    - Avoidance driven by unreliable data (legitimate) or uncomfortable data (sacred value protection)?
    - Distinguishes 'methodologically flawed' from 'morally unacceptable to cite'?
- **Heretical Counterfactuals** — Applying causal reasoning to sacred narratives triggers cleansing. Thinker refuses 'what if things had gone differently?' about foundational assumptions.
  - Key questions:
    - Causal claims stress-tested with counterfactual reasoning?
    - Foundational assumptions treated as beyond questioning?
    - Distinguishes empirically well-supported from simply not open to debate?
- **Social-Functionalist Mode Detection** — Operating as Scientist (accuracy), Politician (accountability management), Theologian (sacred value protection), or Prosecutor (norm enforcement)?
  - Key questions:
    - Optimized for accuracy (scientist) or audience management (politician)?
    - Certain conclusions protected from revision (theologian)?
    - Prosecuting a case rather than investigating (prosecutor)?
    - Accountability structure promotes self-critical thinking or ingratiation?

#### Decision rules
- IF work refuses clearly relevant trade-off, THEN Taboo Trade-off Avoidance. Analysis incomplete by design.
- IF relevant base rates available but omitted without methodological justification, THEN Forbidden Base Rate avoidance.
- IF causal claims never stress-tested with 'what if?', THEN Heretical Counterfactual avoidance.
- IF overall mode is prosecutorial (building case, not investigating), THEN Prosecutor Mode. Conclusions preceded analysis.

### Accountability Framework
**Source**: Tetlock (1985, 1992); Lerner & Tetlock (1999) Psychological Bulletin

**Purpose**: Does the accountability structure promote or degrade reasoning quality?

Process accountability (how you decided) promotes systematic, self-critical thinking when audience views unknown. Outcome accountability (what result) can motivate creativity but also defensiveness. Pre-decisional accountability debiases more than post-decisional.

#### Components
- **Accountability Type** — Process = decision quality focus. Outcome = results focus.
  - Key questions:
    - Shows reasoning process or just conclusions?
    - Assumptions exposed for scrutiny or hidden?
    - Optimizing for being right (outcome) or reasoning well (process)?
- **Audience Awareness Effects** — Known audience views produce ingratiation. Unknown views produce self-critical thinking.
  - Key questions:
    - Analysis shaped by what audience wants to hear?
    - Uncomfortable conclusions present or pre-filtered?
    - Pre-emptive self-censorship on sensitive topics?
- **Coping Strategy Detection** — Under pressure: attitude shifting/ingratiation, pre-emptive self-criticism, defensive bolstering, decision evasion (buckpassing, procrastination, obfuscation), protest, loophole exploitation, exit.
  - Key questions:
    - Coping strategies visible?
    - Defensive bolstering (strengthening prior position rather than updating)?
    - Decision evasion (hedging so much no actionable conclusion)?
    - Ingratiation (conclusions align with powerful stakeholders)?

#### Decision rules
- IF assumptions hidden and methodology opaque, THEN low process accountability. Cannot be scrutinized, cannot be trusted.
- IF conclusions align with powerful stakeholders' preferences, THEN flag potential ingratiation. Cross-check evidence quality.
- IF hedging so aggressively no actionable conclusion emerges, THEN decision evasion. Coping strategy, not genuine uncertainty.
